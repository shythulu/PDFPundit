# SRC-0401: human-in-the-loop CID→Unicode recovery (Stefanovitch, 2022)

**Why it matters.**
- It is the only openly readable follow-up by a co-author of the DAS 2018 seed (SRC-0400; zero OpenCitations citers).
- It supplies **real-world prevalence** of encoding damage, together with a **natural-pair source**. Charter §3(a) needs both.

## Problem framing
- Born-digital PDFs whose CID→Unicode map is corrupt. Recovery reduces to recovering the translation table (CLM-0417).
- Scanned PDFs are out of scope.
- Real corrupted maps include **many-to-one** entries: a digit and a Cyrillic letter share one code point (CLM-0427).
- The documents are set in ad-hoc, unpublished fonts (CLM-0426).

## Real-world data (verified)
- UDHR corpus: 526 translations. 108 (20%) are image-only and **21 (4%) are corrupted born-digital** (CLM-0415).
- Many of these have independent Unicode texts at unicode.org/udhr (CLM-0416). They are candidate **natural pairs** (GAP-202). Each pair must be verified before use.

## Method
1. **Automatic step.**
   - Space = the CID that appears on the most lines.
   - Full stop = the most frequent line-final CID where the line ends 20–80% of the way across (not at the margin).
2. **Interactive step.** The user types words they can read. The system matches token-length *sequences* against the CID stream: 5-word sequences are ≥97% unique. It then fills the map.
3. **Effort.** Full recovery needs 4–5% of the words typed (CLM-0421). 2 of 4 documents were fully recovered (CLM-0424).

## Limitations and future work (verified)
- **Statistics.** The author names statistical n-gram decipherment of CID strings as possible, but has no language models for endangered languages (CLM-0418, CLM-0419). For high-resource languages the route is untried (GAP-205).
- **Devanagari.** The pre-base i-matra is stored after the consonant but drawn before it. Extraction follows glyph position, so the order must be repaired (CLM-0422, GAP-203).
- **Mongolian.** Vertical text cannot be split into lines by the extraction tool (CLM-0423).
- **Future work.** Train OCR for any script from Unicode-chart glyphs with augmentation (CLM-0425).
- **Code.** The tool was "not yet available" at publication.

## Our reading
- The DAS 2018 system is described here as OCR over "well supported languages" with extensive training (CLM-0420). Glyph-OCR recovery therefore does not cover low-resource scripts or ad-hoc fonts.
- The human-in-the-loop design fits forensic practice: every user-typed anchor is attributable, so the mappings are provenance grade 4 with a named source. An automated product would still need a confidence and abstention path (GAP-204).
