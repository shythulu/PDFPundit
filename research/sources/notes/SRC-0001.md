# SRC-0001 — REPDF (the state of the art PDFPundit starts from)

**Why it matters:** PDFPundit's entire engine design (`nimbalyst-local/plans/pdfpundit-technical-design.md`)
reimplements this paper in Rust. It is the baseline to replicate, and its stated limits are the
first gap seeds.

## Method (7 steps)
1. **Template PDFs as a font DB**: Word documents that contain every supported language are
   exported to PDF. Each subset `/FontFile2` is swapped for the full font, and `W`/`Widths` are
   filled from `hmtx`.
2. **Byte-level scan** for `obj`/`endobj`/`stream`/`endstream`, grouping objects by `/Type`.
3. **Page information**: `/Contents` and `/MediaBox` per `/Page`.
4. **Inflate and classify streams**: by `/Subtype /Image`, or by operators (`BT`/`ET`/`Tf`/`Tj` for text, `Do` for images).
5. **Resources are copied into the template**. Images that can't be placed are saved as separate files (CLM-0015).
6. **Font mapping**:
   - If `/BaseFont` survives, the DB is looked up by name.
   - Otherwise, hex codes are decoded through each candidate font's glyphOrder and cmap.
     Each candidate is scored against 6-language dictionaries (bonus for real words; penalties
     for mixed language and rare characters). A surviving `/ToUnicode` becomes a custom dictionary.
7. **Output** is emitted through pikepdf, which rebuilds the xref (CLM-0018).

No machine learning. There is no carving from disk or memory images, even though carving is the stated motivation.

## Evaluation
- **Corpus**: 50 Word documents × {Save As, Print to PDF} × C1–C10 = 1,000 files (SRC-0002).
  Six languages, 6 pages each. Text is GPT-generated. Single synthetic fault per file.
- **Metric**: exact word match against Google Document AI OCR of the original (CLM-0016).
  Rendering was checked in Chrome only (CLM-0021).
- **Baselines**: iLovePDF, Repairit, PDF24. No open-source engines (CLM-0017).
- **Results**:
  - Mean 90.67% (CLM-0001). C1–C3: 100%; C4–C5: ≥99.7%.
  - C6: about 88%, but Arabic only 33–40%.
  - C9: about 60% (CLM-0004).
  - C10: Save As 99.57% vs Print to PDF 35.35% (CLM-0002). The authors' explanation is an untested hypothesis (CLM-0003).

## Stated limitations and future work (all verified quotes)
- CLM-0006 / CLM-0007: single-fault synthetic damage; asks for real-world or realistic damage datasets.
- CLM-0008 / CLM-0009: Word-only corpus; asks for other producers and for scanned documents.
- CLM-0010 / CLM-0011: font-DB coverage; asks for an extensible font DB.
- CLM-0012 / CLM-0013 / CLM-0014: Arabic dictionary scoring; asks for AI-based contextual font choice.

## Our critique (not quotes; CLM-0022 to CLM-0025)
- The per-class numbers are internally inconsistent across tables. Replicate before quoting them as targets.
- Font inference assumes glyph IDs are stable and ignores GSUB. That is a plausible root cause
  of the Arabic failures. Type1, CFF and Type3 fonts are unhandled.
- The metric is OCR-based, so it can't tell *extractable* text from merely *visible* text. It
  has no fidelity or fabrication measure, even though the method inserts synthesized fonts (CLM-0020).
- Untested damage: fragmentation, mid-file holes, head truncation, compound faults, damaged
  ObjStm or xref streams, incremental updates, encryption and signatures.
