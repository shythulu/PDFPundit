# SRC-0205 — Multi-parser error messages as a compliance test

**Why it matters:** it gives an expert-labelled real-world non-compliance rate for crawled PDFs and
states the LangSec objection to lenient repair: without normative repair rules, divergent repairs
are an attack surface.

## Data and method
- SafeDocs test-and-evaluation data: *internet_sourced* (a Common Crawl subset) and a hand-curated
  *dangerous* set (CLM-0260). Every file was manually marked valid or rejected (non-compliant or malicious).
- About 20 parser configurations (caradoc, hammer, mutool, pdfium, pdfminer, pdftk, peepdf, poppler, qpdf,
  veraPDF, xpdf, ...) with 955 error-message regexes form a file x message relation matrix. A Bernoulli pseudo-likelihood ratio flags misclassified files.

## Findings (verified quotes)
- internet_sourced: 9000 files, 1794 rejected (CLM-0261, CLM-0262).
- Many parsers repair non-compliant files (CLM-0264). Different repairs, exploitable by crafted files, led
  the T&E team to reject every non-compliant file (CLM-0263).
- False reporting about 5% for finding bad files among good (CLM-0265). Parser behaviours are largely
  non-redundant (CLM-0266).
- The data include benign correctable malformations, uncorrectable files and malicious files (CLM-0267).

## Limitations / future work (verified)
- Supervised: needs labelled samples. Unsupervised bootstrapping is future work (CLM-0268). The 2023
  follow-up "Unsupervised clustering of file dialects" (doi:10.1109/spw59333.2023.00019) was seen
  in the citation check but not screened.

## Our reading (not evidence)
- 1794/9000 is about 20% of crawled PDFs being non-compliant or malicious by expert judgement. This is a
  real-world prevalence anchor for charter §3(a), though the internet_sourced sampling method is not described.
- The labelled "benign malformation that could be corrected" vs "could not be corrected" split is
  the closest thing we found to a real-world repairability ground truth. Whether the SafeDocs T&E
  data are public is unknown and should be asked of the PDF Association/JPL.
