# SRC-0302: pugz

**Why it matters:** not a recovery paper. It solves the same subproblems as recovery after a lost
start: finding DEFLATE block boundaries without context, and decoding with an unknown history. It
is engineered and measured on 100 real files (193 GB), and its successor rapidgzip (SRC-0303)
hardens the block finder.

## Method
- **Block-start detection:** try every bit offset with strict fail-early checks; after a success,
  decode five more blocks to confirm synchronization (CLM-0322).
- **Undetermined context:** the 32 KB window starts as unique symbols U0..U32767, so every
  back-reference into the unknown history is traceable and resolvable later (CLM-0323). This is the
  same idea as Brown's co-indices.
- Model of how quickly undetermined characters wash out, from literal density and match offsets.

## Results
- With an unknown context in real FASTQ files, 72% (normal level) and 37% (highest level) of
  sequences are fully determined after the first sequence-resolved block (CLM-0324). Higher
  compression levels keep unknown bytes alive longer (long back-reference chains).

## Limitations and future work
- Block detection is tuned to ASCII; binary data needs more careful boundary detection (CLM-0325).
  PDF font programs and images are binary.
- Guessing the undetermined characters was not explored (CLM-0326).

## Transfer to PDF (our inference)
- The five-block confirmation is unavailable for most PDF streams (64% single-block, OBS-0301). A
  PDF resynchronizer must confirm a candidate some other way: end-of-block at the known end of the
  stream (the `endstream` position), Adler-32 over the tail, or content-stream tokenisation.
- The unique-symbol window gives exact per-byte provenance for free: every output byte is either
  a literal (recovered) or a traced unknown. It maps directly to grade 1 vs "unknown".
