# SRC-0301: Brown 2013, ZipRec 1.0

**Why it matters:** the only published method for recovering data *inside* a damaged DEFLATE
block, which is the common case for PDF streams (OBS-0301). Its stated failures are the clearest
evidence we have for GAP-005.

## Method
1. **Intra-block recovery.** Within a block, the Huffman tables survive, so decoding can restart
   at any symbol boundary. After a known corrupt region, all 48 candidate bit offsets (the maximum
   code length) are decoded in parallel until they converge on the same boundary; from then on the
   decode is exact (CLM-0310). Convergence: median under 50 bytes, never over 150 (CLM-0311).
   The history window is cleared to co-indexed unknowns at the restart.
2. **Reconstruction** with forward and backward byte 7/8-gram models; a replacement is accepted only
   with at least one good context and a confidence of at least 0.97 of the best (CLM-0315).
3. **Window realignment.** After reconstructing text past the gap, slide it against the history to
   estimate how many bytes the gap swallowed, then resolve back-references across the gap.

## Results
- Corrupting 128 to 4096 compressed bytes gives output that differs from the original by less
  than twice the bytes the gap represented (CLM-0312); mean ratio about 1.5 across 21 languages.
- Metrics: recall and precision of reconstruction, because wrong bytes mislead where unknown bytes
  do not (CLM-0314). This is the right frame for our fabrication metric.

## Limitations and future work (verified quotes)
- **Corruption usually goes unnoticed by the decoder:** 4 of 100 random 512-byte corruptions made
  the bitstream invalid (CLM-0307). Automatic detection only for runs of at least 128 identical
  bytes (CLM-0308); two general detectors failed because natural score variation exceeds the drop at
  the damage point (CLM-0316, CLM-0317). Our OBS-0300 confirms this on PDF: 84% of damaged C9
  streams decode to the end and fail only Adler-32.
- Lost block header: inferring the Huffman tables was infeasible in isolation (CLM-0318).
- Multiple corruptions per file not evaluated (CLM-0320). C9 hits 11-28 streams per file.
- Repetitive markup tags are the hardest content (CLM-0321), a bad sign for PDF content streams.
- The evaluation damage is a zeroed run at a fixed offset (CLM-0313), which is exactly what the
  detector finds. Real-world source of such runs: imaging tools that zero-fill unreadable sectors
  (CLM-0309, supports DMG-002).

## Our reading (inference)
- The intra-block resync is **concrete enough to test on PDF streams** once the damage position is
  known (HYP-151). Localisation is the missing piece (GAP-150).
- ZipRec is GPL: specs may describe the behaviour, never the code.
