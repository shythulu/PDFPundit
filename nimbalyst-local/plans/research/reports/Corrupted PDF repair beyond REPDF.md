# Measured corruption rewrites PDFPundit's repair playbook

There is little published work on PDF repair after REPDF, and what there is points one way. No indexed paper cites REPDF yet. Its only direct rival is CPR, a paper in the same DFRWS issue. The useful progress comes from older DEFLATE-forensics work (Brown's ZipRec, Barral's checksum-oracle search), from glyph-decipherment research, and from the repair code inside qpdf, MuPDF, pdf.js, PDFium and lopdf. The most important finding for PDFPundit is that **its C9 plan targets damage the REPDF corpus does not contain**. Each of the 100 public C9 files has **12–30 random-byte replacements (mean 21.6)**: one per damaged Flate stream, plus about a third that land outside Flate data. **Only 3.1% are single-bit flips.** 84% of damaged streams decode to the end and fail only the Adler-32 check. The design's search tries 8 bit flips per byte in a window around the inflate error, so it would repair about 3% of streams. A checkpointed search over all 255 byte values in pure-Rust `miniz_oxide`, with localizers to narrow the search, repaired **98.6% of inflate-error streams and 94.5% of grammar-flagged text streams byte-exactly**. A second finding is just as consequential: font inference built on bundled Noto glyph maps cannot decode REPDF's documents. They are typeset in 44 mostly Google-Fonts and Office faces, and in "Print to PDF" files the only font names are anonymous `CIDFont+Fn` tags. A third finding came later: **Microsoft Print to PDF draws 4.7% of the glyphs in its half of the corpus as vector outlines**, so no font, ToUnicode or text-layer metric can see them, while REPDF's Google Document AI OCR metric counts them. Three more changes follow. The carver should adopt invariants that mature engines already use. Evaluation must match REPDF's OCR metric and use group-aware splits. Hand-set constants should give way to generate-and-validate selection and risk-calibrated deferral. Common real-world damage falls outside C1–C10 altogether, so PDFPundit needs held-out corruptors or it will overfit REPDF's generator.

## REPDF stands nearly alone, and its taxonomy misses real damage

### One paper, one concurrent rival, and a foundational literature

REPDF evaluates on **1,000 files: 50 Word-authored base documents × 10 corruption scenarios × 2 creation methods ("Save As" and "Print to PDF")**. Each six-page document repeats the same GPT-5-generated content on each page in a different language (en, zh, hi, es, fr, ar). Text recovery is **word recall against Google Document AI OCR of the rendered original** ([REPDF §5.2–5.3](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)). As of September 2026 Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex both list **zero citations** ([Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.fsidi.2026.302061?fields=title,year,citationCount,citations.title); [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.fsidi.2026.302061)). REPDF clearly beats commercial tools on the font classes. Its margin is small or negative on the stream and truncation classes:

| Class (REPDF Table 5, text %) | REPDF Save As / Print | Best commercial Save As / Print | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| C6 font map lost | 88.12 / 88.13 | 40.90 / 5.00 | Large win from the font DB; Arabic only 33–40% (Table 2) |
| C8 FontFile2 + ToUnicode deleted | 99.51 / 87.57 | 42.42 / 16.00 | "Save As" keeps real font names; "Print to PDF" names are `CIDFont+F1` |
| C9 zlib byte modified | 58.77 / 62.70 | 58.66 / 60.43 (PDF24) | At most 2.3 points better than PDF24 |
| C10 truncated to 70% | 99.58 / 30.49 | 70.53 / 31.52 | "Print to PDF" loses to two commercial tools |

The paper's tables do not agree exactly with each other. Table 2's per-language means give C10 "Print to PDF" **35.35%**, the figure the text quotes in §5.4. Table 5's tool comparison gives **30.49%**. C9 is 60.11% in Table 2 but 58.77/62.70 in Table 5. Both sets are the paper's own numbers from different tables, and both are used in the research notes. Any claim to "beat REPDF" should therefore re-run REPDF's metric rather than quote one figure ([REPDF Tables 2 and 5](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)).

**One note conflict needs resolving.** The literature note found "no 2024–2026 academic paper specifically on PDF repair other than REPDF". The font note found **CPR (Kim et al., FSI:DI 56, 2026)**, a concurrent paper in the same DFRWS issue. The literature note's claim is superseded. CPR combines carving with a 40-font FontDB and uses Llama 3.1 8B as a yes/no "naturalness" validator. When both ToUnicode and FontFile were removed, it recovered **0.333 (CER 0.048)** against 0.000 for baseline tools. When resources objects were corrupted, it recovered **1.000**. On MS Print to PDF files it reached **100% (CER 0.030)**, because it indexes font tags per page ([CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf)). CPR and REPDF define recovery differently, so their C8-like numbers (33% vs ~88–99%) cannot be ranked against each other.

The rest of the relevant literature is older and aimed at specific techniques. **Cohen (2007)** won the DFRWS 2007 carving challenge with PDF-structure validators ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.diin.2007.10.001)). **Chen et al. (2008)** added validators for entropy at sector seams, trial inflation across candidate seams, and PDF-token tables. They scored **100% on fragmented PDFs but 25% on "broken" ones with missing pieces** ([Chen 2008](https://seclab.hdu.edu.cn/static/uploads/paper/08-13.pdf)). Garfinkel's "known offset" bifragment carving is the direct analogue of using xref offsets to reassemble fragments ([Garfinkel 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1742287607000369)). For DEFLATE, **Brown's ZipRec** recovers the tail of a block after a corrupt span using the block's known Huffman tables. Its output errors are only **1.44–1.65× the corrupted span** ([Brown 2013](https://dfrws.org/sites/default/files/session-files/2013_USA_paper-improved_recovery_and_reconstruction_of_deflated_files.pdf)). **Barral et al.** used Adler-32 as an oracle over bit-flip candidates and raised recovered data from **8.32% to 99.68%**. Their two-flip search took **22 days to over a year per fragment** ([Barral 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00856)). For fonts, **Vol/Krutko et al. (2018)** decoded corrupted encodings with a per-glyph CNN plus a language model ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Automatic-Recovery-of-Corrupted-Font-Encoding-in-Vol-Krutko/e0c6171f58eef6f5b7ef5b388d8c56868e8f5519)). Commercial tools publish no algorithms. Success rates such as "Stellar 87%" appear only in listicles whose source could not be fetched, so this report ignores them ([PDFTEQ list](https://pdfteq.com/blog/posts/best-pdf-repair-tools-2026)).

### Real-world malformation is common, varied and outside C1–C10

Across roughly 8M Common Crawl PDFs, **25% raise at least one Arlington grammar error and iText rebuilt the xref for 3.1%**. **44% use xref streams, 23% are hybrid-xref files and 28% are linearized** ([Allison 2022](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)). A strict parser accepted only **14.65% of 10,000 web PDFs**, and the same crawl found **incremental updates in 64% of parsed files** ([Caradoc](https://paperstreet.picty.org/yeye/resources/conf-spw-EndignouxLM16/document.pdf)). Truncation is routine: the December 2019 crawl truncated **22% of PDFs** at its 1 MB cap ([Allison et al. 2020](http://spw20.langsec.org/papers/corpus_LangSec2020.pdf)). **13.5% of GovDocs1 PDFs render inconsistently** in at least one popular reader ([Kuchta et al. 2018](http://srg.doc.ic.ac.uk/files/papers/pdf-emse-18.pdf)).

Reported bad-xref rates range from 0.5% (23 of 3,977 files, about half due to junk before `%PDF-`) to iText's 3.1% ([Eliot Jones 2025](https://eliot-jones.com/2025/8/pdf-parsing-xref)). The figures differ because one counts bad xref declarations and the other counts any rebuild; they do not contradict each other. Real xrefs are usually *wrong* rather than *missing*. Examples include a producer that never counted image-stream bytes, and a `startxref` that was off by one byte, which prompted qpdf's "almost always worth it" nearby search ([qpdf #341](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/issues/341)).

**No study publishes a census of what causes the damage in files users report as corrupted.** That gap is real. Measured against the prevalence data above, REPDF's classes are single-fault and Word-only. They omit object-stream and xref-stream loss, broken incremental-update chains (including a truncated last revision), linearized files cut after the first-page section, CR/LF transfer damage, zeroed sectors mid-file, fragmented or out-of-order carves, loss of the file head, wrong `/Length`, damage in non-Flate filters and in PNG predictors, non-TrueType fonts, damaged `/Encrypt`, and multi-fault files.

The gzip FAQ attributes "99.9% of the problems with gzip" to ASCII-mode transfers. That is a precedent for undoing global CR/LF damage, with Adler-32 as the per-stream oracle ([gzip.org](https://www.gzip.org/ancient/)). Out-of-order fragmentation is common on modern NTFS: 38–44% of fragmented image files ([van der Meer 2021](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2021_APAC_paper-a_contemporary_investigation_of_ntfs_file_fragmentation.pdf)).

A scan of the public corpus's originals, run while writing this report, shows that it exercises less structure than its size suggests. **All 50 "Save As" originals use xref streams and object streams, but all 300 page dictionaries and every Font/FontDescriptor dictionary sit at top level.** **All 50 "Print to PDF" originals use classic xref tables** ([REPDF dataset](https://github.com/dfrc-korea/REPDF)). REPDF's 100% on C2 and C5 therefore says nothing about producers that pack pages and fonts into ObjStms. That is the case where pdf.js and lopdf fail and PDFium succeeds by expanding every candidate stream ([lopdf #449](https://github.com/J-F-Liu/lopdf/issues/449)).

**REPDF's corpus is the only public one that pairs corrupted files with their originals.** A second source of pairs exists and costs nothing. CC-MAIN-2021-31-PDF-UNTRUNCATED refetched about 2M PDFs that Common Crawl had flagged as truncated. Its provenance table gives each file's WARC byte range, so every refetched file forms a **natural truncated/complete pair** across many producers ([Digital Corpora](https://digitalcorpora.org/corpora/file-corpora/cc-main-2021-31-pdf-untruncated/constructing-the-cc-main-2021-31-pdf-untruncated-corpus/)). Before using a pair, compare hashes of the first 1 MB, because the site may have served a different file on refetch. The SafeDocs Stressful corpus (32,679 issue-tracker files) and the pdf.js and qpdf test suites serve as no-crash robustness sets ([PDF Association](https://pdfa.org/resource/stressful-pdf-corpus)). The DFRWS 2007 image is still the carving benchmark ([DFRWS](https://dfrws.org/forensic-challenges)).

## C9 is twenty random bytes, not one flipped bit

**The conflict and its resolution.** REPDF §5.1.9 says C9 is "realized by modifying a random byte within a zlib-compressed stream" ([REPDF](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)). PDFPundit's design reads that as "the corpus's C9 flips exactly one byte" and builds its fixture and brute force on it ([design §4.5, §8, §16](../../pdfpundit-technical-design.md)). The published files contradict that reading.

Part A of [c9exp.py](../experiments/c9exp.py) diffs the 100 C9 files against their originals, and its `partA.json` output was re-tallied for this report. Every file differs in **12–30 bytes (mean 21.62)** at unchanged length. **1,445 of 2,162 changes (66.8%) are inside Flate data, exactly one per damaged stream**, across **7–26 damaged streams per file (mean 14.45)** out of about 34. The other third falls in dictionaries and object headers (`/Length`→`\x1cength`, `115 0 obj`→`11l 0 obj`), in uncompressed XMP and CMap text, and in DCT image data. Hamming weights run from 1 to 8 bits. **Only 68 changes (3.1%) are single-bit**, which is exactly what uniform random replacement predicts (8/255) ([REPDF dataset](https://github.com/dfrc-korea/REPDF)).

The measurement is the supported account. The paper's sentence holds only per damaged stream, where one random byte is replaced. "Flip" is wrong twice over: the changes are not single-bit flips, and there is not one per file. REPDF's corruptor code is not published, so the rule that also hits bytes outside Flate data is inferred from the diffs, not read from source. The selection note's "C9 flips exactly one byte" and the literature note's "a plausible bit-rot model" inherited the design's reading and are superseded.

**Why the planned ladder misses.** Byte-fed inflate of every damaged stream shows **1,220 of 1,445 (84.4%) decode to the end and fail only Adler-32**. **221 (15.3%) raise an inflate error**, a median of **5 bytes** after the damage, and 99.1% of those fall inside the planned [k − 4096, k + 64] window ([c9exp.py](../experiments/c9exp.py), Part A). The synthetic replication in Part B explains why errors are rare. Across 1,500 random replacements, inflate errors fired **44.5% of the time in the first 4 KiB of output, 10.9% in 4–32 KiB, and 0.5% beyond 32 KiB**. Nearly all detections are "distance too far back", an error that can only fire while the history is shorter than 32 KiB.

**For real damage in large streams, then, inflate is effectively silent and Adler-32 is the only alarm.** A window keyed to an error offset is wrong for the majority case. The design's step 3 flips 8 bits per byte, which covers 3% of modifications. Its step 4 resyncs at stored blocks, and the block census in [blocks.py](../experiments/blocks.py) finds **exactly one stored block in 3,398 streams**. **94% of text content streams and 100% of CMaps are a single dynamic block.**

**What works.** The benchmark in [mzbench](../experiments/mzbench/src/main.rs) clones `miniz_oxide`'s `DecompressorOxide` every 256 input bytes. It resumes each of the 255 candidate values at each position from the nearest checkpoint and accepts only on an Adler-verified `Done`. For [szcheck](../experiments/szcheck/src/main.rs), a checkpoint is 10,504 bytes; the streaming `InflateState` is 43,296 bytes. Results by stream type follow.

On inflate-error streams, scanning backward from k, the search **fixed 218 of 221 (98.6%), all byte-exact, with a median of 13.5 ms and p90 of 129 ms**. On Adler-only streams of 4 KiB or less, a full-range search accepted **245 of 245 (243 exact), median 0.23 s**. The [grammar_loc.py](../experiments/grammar_loc.py) localizer flags the first ungrammatical content or CMap token in **85%** of Adler-only text streams. The true damage lies within [flag − 64, flag + 8] in 91.8% of those. Searching [flag − 256, flag + 8] fixed **324 of 343 flagged streams (94.5%), median 25 ms**.

Fonts are the hard remainder. They are **798 of the 1,220 Adler-only streams**, with a median of 16.2 KiB compressed. An unlocalized search finished only **66% of 4–16 KiB streams within 20 s**. The fix is a TrueType table-checksum localizer. All 12,532 tables in the 854 intact fonts had valid checksums. The first table whose checksum fails contains the damage in **765 of 766** damaged fonts, and it averages 36.9% of the font ([OpenType checksum rule](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/typography/opentype/otspec181/otff)). No script for this localizer (E6) is committed, and its search cost is unmeasured.

Acceptance must be strict because **Adler-32 is weak on short data** ([RFC 3309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3309)). One accept in 245 small streams was a genuine collision on 2.7 KB of output. Another came from damage to the Adler trailer itself. The design's alternative, "or classifies as valid content", would generate false accepts at scale.

| Ladder element | Current design (§4.5, §16) | Measured on REPDF C9 | Change to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output on Adler mismatch | lost unless an error yields `Prefix` | keeping all output lifts mean correct bytes from **48.1% to 59.0%**; same-length Adler-only streams are a median **99.99%** correct | new `ChecksumMismatch{data}` state, always the fallback |
| Search alphabet | 8 bit flips per byte | covers 3.1% of changes | all 255 values |
| Window | [k − 4096, k + 64] | right only for the 15% of streams with an inflate error | backward from k with slack k + 8; grammar window; first bad TrueType table; full range if ≤ 4 KiB |
| Acceptance | Adler-32 *or* "valid content" | Adler false accepts: 1/245 and 1/324 | Adler required; also test neighbouring values and positions; tie-break by grammar or checksum; otherwise `Partial` with the differing bytes marked |
| Resync | next stored block | 1 in 3,398 streams contains one | Brown-style intra-block resync with the known Huffman tables and unknown-window markers |
| Budget | 256 K attempts, streams ≤ 256 KiB | one window = 1.04 M candidates; median hit after ~1.3 K | per-stream time budget (~2 s default, ~20 s deep), rayon across the ~14.5 damaged streams per file |

Together these changes make **about 39% of all damaged streams byte-exact on demonstrated numbers**, compared with at most 3% under the current plan. Adding the font localizer plausibly takes this to 60–70%. That figure is an estimate, not a measurement. All gains are counted in stream bytes. Their effect on REPDF's OCR word metric was not measured.

The result also carries an overfitting warning. The 255-value search works because REPDF's generator changes one byte per stream. Real bursts and zeroed sectors need Brown's resync, which the ladder should keep as its general-purpose fallback. Brown's method needs a custom inflater that emits literal-or-marker symbols. rapidgzip's 15-bit window markers are the reference design, and no Rust crate implements them yet ([rapidgzip](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08955v1)).

A third of C9's changes fall outside Flate data. So the inflate probe must not depend on `/Length` surviving, and the lexer must accept near-miss tokens such as `11l 0 obj`.

**C10 is a font problem more than a DEFLATE problem.** Every C10 file is cut at exactly **70.0%**. In "Save As" files, all content streams and images lie before the cut. In "Print to PDF" files, **35.6% of text content-stream bytes and 45.7% of DCT image bytes lie after it**. That sets a ceiling of about 64% by content bytes, well above REPDF's 35.35%. **92 of the 97 streams that straddle the cut are fonts** (E8 census; no script committed).

The corpus scan above adds the missing link. "Print to PDF" files name every font `CIDFont+F1`…`F22`. A tool that loses the font objects therefore cannot even look the font up. The lost points are recovered by salvaging truncated fonts (the tables before the cut, plus glyphs reachable through `loca`) and by identifying fonts without names (§5). Images that straddle the cut need lenient decoding: append `FFD9` to a cut JPEG and decode with [zune-jpeg](https://docs.rs/zune-jpeg/latest/zune_jpeg/), or use pure-Rust [hayro-jpeg2000](https://docs.rs/crate/hayro-jpeg2000/latest) for JPX. hayro-jpeg2000's behaviour on truncated codestreams is unverified.

## Mature parsers already encode the carver's missing invariants

Source reading of nine engines at September 2026 commits found the same core method everywhere: scan for `N G obj`, and let the later copy in the file win. The engines differ in exactly the places where PDFPundit's carver is underspecified.

PDFPundit's dead-range design plus its inflate probe is **stronger than any surveyed engine** at avoiding phantom objects inside streams and at bounding streams. **No engine uses decompression to find a stream's extent.** All fall back to the first `endstream`, and Poppler even overrides a correct `/Length` in reconstructed mode ([Poppler Parser.cc](https://gitlab.freedesktop.org/poppler/poppler/-/blob/c8dc23d564f233875386b7aa1d59fc909ea58799/poppler/Parser.cc#L229)). The probe needs two guards. First, accept its end only if `endstream` follows within EOL slack, because a probe can succeed on a prefix. Second, treat an Adler mismatch as a found extent, since 84% of C9 streams end that way.

Two lopdf rules are worth adopting. lopdf recovers a stream length only when **exactly one** EOL-framed `endstream endobj` exists within the object's bounds ([lopdf](https://github.com/J-F-Liu/lopdf/blob/0e781ce05f083330ecc24ac8c67833fbd076e7a2/src/parser/mod.rs#L344)). It also accepts object headers only at the start of a line. That second rule exists because literal strings and comments inside *non-stream* objects can contain convincing `12 0 obj` text. PDFPundit's §14.2 loop skips only headers inside streams, so it would still promote those ([lopdf](https://github.com/J-F-Liu/lopdf/blob/0e781ce05f083330ecc24ac8c67833fbd076e7a2/src/reader.rs#L1452)). qpdf adds a cross-check: it rejects a recovered length that would swallow another object's header ([qpdf](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/blob/54d6053af283bbeb8b325f4886c0f65cc51f2b80/libqpdf/QPDF_objects.cc#L1440)).

**Duplicate resolution needs a quality gate, not just byte order.** MuPDF refuses to let "a truncated object at EOF overwrite a good one" ([MuPDF](https://github.com/ArtifexSoftware/mupdf/blob/d587982f971e2404e72b0539b2d9c60ad2c9b03f/source/pdf/pdf-repair.c#L102)). pdf.js lets a later copy win only if it parses ([pdf.js](https://github.com/mozilla/pdf.js/blob/d52fdf411a6e4d338180687456e0df019e28475e/src/core/xref.js#L526)). Under the design's "last in byte order wins", a truncated incremental update would shadow complete earlier revisions. That is exactly the C10 case.

The rule should be **last *well-formed* copy wins, with each generation a distinct key**. For object-stream members, adopt MuPDF's bug-708286 rule: a compressed member takes its container's byte offset, so a later uncompressed copy beats it ([MuPDF](https://github.com/ArtifexSoftware/mupdf/blob/d587982f971e2404e72b0539b2d9c60ad2c9b03f/source/pdf/pdf-repair.c#L277)).

The design treats xref streams as "diagnosis only". That discards the trailer dictionary MuPDF, PDFium, pdf.js, qpdf and pypdf all harvest (Root, Info, ID, Encrypt, Size). Root selection should validate candidates the way pdf.js and hayro do: Root must lead to a Pages dict, which must lead to a Kids array or integer Count ([hayro](https://github.com/LaurenzV/hayro/blob/ae09437b4e5939fb76125ed8c5ed3c38d6d6ac09/hayro-syntax/src/xref.rs#L178)). Declared offsets are noisy evidence, not garbage. lopdf 0.45 corrects `startxref` and `/Prev` within ±64 bytes ([lopdf](https://github.com/J-F-Liu/lopdf/blob/0e781ce05f083330ecc24ac8c67833fbd076e7a2/src/reader.rs#L1645)). qpdf rebases every offset on a header found anywhere in the first 1 KiB, which fixes the junk-prefix case behind half of Jones's bad xrefs ([qpdf](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/blob/54d6053af283bbeb8b325f4886c0f65cc51f2b80/libqpdf/QPDF_objects.cc#L218)).

One design rule is also stricter than current practice. §20 rejects a bare CR after `stream`, but qpdf accepts it because "some readers, including Adobe reader, accept a carriage return by itself", and hayro accepts it too ([qpdf](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/blob/54d6053af283bbeb8b325f4886c0f65cc51f2b80/libqpdf/QPDF_objects.cc#L1381)). Accept a bare CR with a Finding. Before `endstream`, strip exactly one EOL, never all trailing whitespace.

For the page tree, only hayro brute-forces pages that the tree does not reach. Its test, any dictionary with `/Contents`, would also admit annotations ([hayro](https://github.com/LaurenzV/hayro/blob/ae09437b4e5939fb76125ed8c5ed3c38d6d6ac09/hayro-syntax/src/page.rs#L68)). PDFPundit's byte-order orphan fallback should score candidates instead. Require `/Type /Page`, or all of: no `/Kids`, `/Contents` resolving to streams, and a `/MediaBox` or `/Parent`. Exclude annotation subtypes.

qpdf adds four more protections worth copying ([qpdf](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/blob/54d6053af283bbeb8b325f4886c0f65cc51f2b80/libqpdf/QPDF_pages.cc#L52)). It climbs `/Parent` when the catalog's `/Pages` points at a page. It drops a page that appears twice in recovery mode, and a page with more than two errors. It keeps visited sets over `/Kids` arrays as well as nodes. And it caps depth at 100.

**Robustness bugs in repair code follow a few recurring patterns.** Object numbers taken from ObjStm headers have overflowed ([CVE-2012-5340](https://www.exploit-db.com/exploits/23246)). Object streams have referred to themselves recursively ([CVE-2018-6544](https://app.opencve.io/cve/CVE-2018-6544)). Reconstruction has recursed into itself. qpdf 12.2 now ignores recovered arrays and dicts with more than 5,000 elements ([qpdf release notes](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/blob/54d6053af283bbeb8b325f4886c0f65cc51f2b80/manual/release-notes.rst)). One Rust crate set `/Size` to the number of objects rather than max id + 1, which silently redefined objects on the next incremental save ([pdf_oxide #1742](https://github.com/yfedoseev/pdf_oxide/issues/1742)).

Rust removes the memory-safety class of bug. The denial-of-service and wrong-object classes remain. PDFPundit therefore needs checked ids, bounded ObjStm headers, caps on objects, trailers, elements, inflated bytes and warnings, cycle-safe walks, and byte-order determinism independent of hash maps or rayon. Keeping every shadowed revision, and flagging content appended after a signature's `/ByteRange`, turns "last wins" into forensic evidence. Shadow attacks fooled **16 of 29 viewers** ([NDSS 2021](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/shadow-attacks-hiding-and-replacing-content-in-signed-pdfs/)).

## Font recovery must decode the document's own fonts, not Noto's

The design decodes `code → gid → gmap(Noto).unicode(gid)`, and "the inference path always draws candidates from the bundled DB" ([design §5.2, §5.5, §18.2](../../pdfpundit-technical-design.md)). For Identity-H subsets, the gids belong to the *original* font. The same corpus scan counted **44 distinct `/BaseFont` names** in the "Save As" originals. The most common are Cambria (45 files), Rubik-Medium (37), Bricolage Grotesque Light (35), Oswald-Medium (34), NotoSerif-Thin (34) and Poppins-Bold (32), along with Chinese display faces such as MaoKenWangXingYuan and zihunbiantaoti. **Only two are Noto faces.** "Print to PDF" files carry only `CIDFont+F1`–`F22` ([REPDF dataset](https://github.com/dfrc-korea/REPDF)). This matches the paper's statement that it chose "five representative fonts for each language" ([REPDF §5.2](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)).

**A Noto-only glyph-map database therefore cannot reproduce REPDF's C6 and C8 numbers.** REPDF and CPR both work because their databases contain the corpus's actual fonts; CPR says so explicitly ([CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf)). Most of these faces are openly licensed Google Fonts, so building glyph-map metadata for them is feasible. Glyph maps can also be built at runtime from installed system fonts through the fontique/read-fonts stack already planned for output.

Before any statistical inference, PDFPundit should run the deterministic ISO 32000 §9.10.2 ladder that pdf.js and MuPDF implement. First, a surviving ToUnicode. Next, `/Encoding` and `/Differences` names through the Adobe Glyph List, with pdf.js's `uniXXXX` and `gNN` heuristics. Then the Adobe `-UCS2` collection maps for non-Identity orderings. Finally, when the font program survives, reverse the program's own `cmap` and `post` tables. MuPDF lists that last step as an unimplemented TODO ([MuPDF pdf-unicode.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArtifexSoftware/mupdf/master/source/pdf/pdf-unicode.c); [pdf.js evaluator](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mozilla/pdf.js/master/src/core/evaluator.js)).

REPDF's C6 is defined as deleting `/Font` from a page's `/Resources`, so the font objects survive as orphans ([REPDF §5.1.6](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)). C6 is therefore mainly a *re-linking* problem. Assignment should be per (page, slot), because MS Print to PDF reuses `F1` for different fonts on different pages. Page-indexed maps are how CPR reached 100% on those files.

**Widths are the most underused evidence.** [widths.py](../experiments/widths.py) measured four fonts. It found that font pairs disagree on **51–72 of 73 common characters**, so a dozen used codes fingerprint a font and its gid space. Widths also cut English letter uncertainty from **4.18 bits to 2.10** (Arial-metric) and **1.88** (Times-metric), and to 0.28–0.57 bits for fonts with more varied widths. They carry **no information for Han**: all 20,940 WenQuanYi Han glyphs are 1000 units wide. The script hardcodes the research container's font paths.

For fonts that match nothing (Quartz-renumbered subsets, Type3, unknown faces), treat the code sequence as a substitution cipher. Neural-LM beam search reaches a symbol error rate of **5.8% at 32 characters and 0.07% at 64** ([Aldarrab & May, citing Kambhatla 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.15229v1)). A 6-gram beam search gets **4.13%, where the exact 3-gram solution gets 38.6%** ([Nuhn 2013](https://aclanthology.org/P13-1154)). Below roughly 28 characters, the unicity distance, several decodings stay plausible ([Unicity distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicity_distance)). Word boundaries and width classes shrink that limit in PDFs; no study has measured by how much.

Arabic and Hindi break the one-glyph-one-character assumption behind `gid:u16 → unicode:u32` and `build_tounicode(&BTreeMap<u16,char>)`. Lam-alef and conjunct glyphs map to several code points. Visual-order runs need reversing, except inside ligature clusters. Devanagari reordering cannot be expressed in ToUnicode at all without `/ActualText` ([PyMuPDF #2199](https://github.com/pymupdf/PyMuPDF/issues/2199); [pdf-glyph-mapping](https://github.com/shreevatsa/pdf-glyph-mapping)). Shaping whole dictionary words at build time, and storing `gid → String` plus a glyph-n-gram lexicon, targets REPDF's weakest cell: Arabic C6/C8 at 33–40%.

The human step should change from "pick a font" to "type what you see". Aligning a few typed words to code sequences achieved full recovery from **4–5% of the text** ([Stefanovitch 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.computel-1.10.pdf)). Shape matching, done by rendering surviving glyph programs and matching them against atlases, is proven: PDF Mirage verified glyphs "just under 100%" accurately ([USENIX 2017](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-markwood.pdf)). It is pure-Rust feasible with skrifa and hayro.

OCR helps only when the page renders with the right glyphs (C6, lost ToUnicode, Type3, outlined text). It cannot help C7 or C8. Where it helps, it works best as an **alignment oracle** that votes a code→character map, not as a text source. The pure-Rust route is tract plus PaddleOCR ONNX models: Devanagari recognition scores **96.44% line accuracy at 7.9 MB**, Arabic v5 81.27% ([PaddleOCR Devanagari](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/devanagari_PP-OCRv3_mobile_rec); [Arabic v5](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/arabic_PP-OCRv5_mobile_rec/blob/main/README.md)). CPR's 8B-LLM validator conflicts with the single-binary, no-C constraint. Its own C8-like result was 33%. Keep it out of v1.

## Some text has no font, and only OCR sees it

Everything in the font pipeline assumes that words reach the page as character codes shown through a font. Microsoft Print to PDF breaks that assumption for two of the corpus's 44 fonts, GmarketSansTTFMedium and BousungEG-Light-GB. It draws every glyph they show as a filled vector path. That is **4.7% of all glyphs (18,439 of 391,720)**: **16.5% on English pages and 25.2% on Chinese pages**, in **43 of the 50** Print to PDF originals. Every page that uses either font is outlined, and no other page is. They are the only two fonts in the corpus whose embedding permission is Preview & Print (OS/2 `fsType` 4); the other 42 are installable or editable, and Print to PDF embeds all of them. Save As files contain no outlines, and the corpus has no Type3 fonts ([glyph_count.py](../experiments/glyph_count.py), [outline_fonts.py](../experiments/outline_fonts.py), [font_fstype.py](../experiments/font_fstype.py), [outline_census.py](../experiments/outline_census.py)). PDF has no native SVG; text that starts as SVG arrives as outlines, Type3 glyph procedures or images, and real-world files will contain all three.

Repair keeps outlined glyphs, since carving copies content streams verbatim, and C6–C8 leave them intact because they use no font; they still need the viewer to draw the rest of the page. Every other stage misses them. The typeless-stream classifier looks for `BT/ET/Tf/Tj`, so an outline-only C5 stream is not recognised as content; REPDF's classifier has the same rule ([REPDF §3.4](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)). Font inference and ToUnicode rebuild have no codes to work on. The planned `hayro-interpret` metric drops these words from both sides, and Markdown export loses them silently.

**OCR recovers them.** [ocr_outlined.py](../experiments/ocr_outlined.py) rendered the English and Chinese pages of all 50 document pairs at 200 dpi and read them with PaddleOCR PP-OCRv4 models (detection 4.75 MB, recognition 10.9 MB). On Print to PDF pages with outlines, the text layer keeps **70.8% of English words and 56.4% of Chinese characters**. OCR of the same pages reaches **87.3% and 99.7%**, against 90.0% and 99.7% for OCR of the Save As originals. English word recall stays near 90% even on clean pages, because the Chinese recognition model merges English words; character recall is 93.5–95.3%, so Latin pages need a Latin model. The gap between OCR recall and text-layer recall (+16.5 and +43.3 points pooled) is a cheap per-page signal of text outside the text layer: with this engine it is positive on 30 of 30 outlined Chinese pages and 20 of 28 outlined English pages, never on a clean page, and it misses English pages where under about 16% of the words are outlined.

REPDF's engine is not Tesseract. It uses "the OCR engine of the Google Cloud Document AI API" on original and repaired files ([REPDF §5.3](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)). It does not state the processor version, whether native PDF parsing was on, or whether it sent PDFs or Chrome renders; its README says Chrome's viewer was used "for all assessments" ([REPDF README](https://github.com/dfrc-korea/REPDF)). Enterprise Document OCR costs $1.50 per 1,000 pages, so a full corpus run of about 6,600 pages costs about $10, and processor versions can be pinned ([Document AI](https://cloud.google.com/document-ai); [processor versions](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/manage-processor-versions)). The harness should run Document AI at release and a local PaddleOCR proxy in CI. In the product, OCR fits as a feature-gated module on pure-Rust `tract-onnx` with models downloaded on first use. It should feed Markdown export, a code→character alignment oracle and a text-versus-visual gate, and it should never alter the forensic output. Details, tables and gaps are in [outlined_text_and_ocr.md](../research_notes/Corrupted%20PDF%20repair%20beyond%20REPDF/outlined_text_and_ocr.md).

## Generate, verify and calibrate instead of trusting constants

The §17.4 selector is a hand-set rule: TemplateAssemble if font classes are present, or if C10 leaves less than 0.60 of objects reachable. Both toolpaths run in seconds, and PDFPundit can re-parse, render and re-diagnose its own output. The algorithm-selection literature therefore points to **generate-and-validate**, not predict-then-act. SATzilla-style prediction pays only when running every candidate is unaffordable ([SATzilla](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.2249)). Rules should decide which toolpaths are *feasible* (the MYCIN lesson: the structure matters more than the weights). Every feasible candidate should then run. A lexicographic verification tuple picks the winner. Evaluate the selector as ASlib does, by the fraction of the gap between the single best toolpath and the per-file oracle that it closes ([ASlib](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1506.02465)).

The verification gates in the design today are strict lopdf reload, hayro render and re-diagnose clean. They are a weak oracle, the same failure automated program repair documented. **104 of 110 "plausible" GenProg patches amounted to deleting functionality** ([Qi et al. 2015](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rinard/paper/issta15.pdf)). The PDF equivalents are dropped pages, blank renders, a C9 `Prefix` treated as complete, and a ToUnicode that is correct under wrong glyphs. Each passes the current gates. Add anti-deletion retention checks measured against what the carver found: pages, text-show operators, images and content bytes. Add blank-page detection. Add cross-candidate agreement: when Resave and TemplateAssemble both pass but disagree on text, defer. Report a **plausible-but-wrong rate**, the share of outputs that pass every gate yet fall below the target recovery.

Confidence needs the same treatment. `conf = 0.5·hit + 0.5·margin` ignores how much text a slot has. Its margin divides by `max(best, ε)` and explodes when penalties drive the best score negative. Replace it with a length-aware posterior: character-LM log-likelihood plus dictionary, width and name evidence, normalized by a softmax. Calibrate it with Platt scaling, since the calibration set will have fewer than about 1,000 slots ([Niculescu-Mizil & Caruana](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~alexn/papers/calibration.icml05.crc.rev3.pdf)). Choose the auto-accept threshold from a target selective risk with a finite-sample bound, in place of the magic 0.35 ([Geifman & El-Yaniv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500)). Certifying at most 2% wrong auto-picks at 95% confidence needs roughly 150 error-free labelled slots, by the rule of three. The "readable text but no embeddable font" case is a policy choice, not uncertainty in a prediction. Handle it as a batched default, not a per-file stop.

**PDFPundit's planned metric is not comparable with REPDF's.** Dice over a Myers alignment, `2·matched/(len_orig + len_repaired)`, is exactly LCS-F1. It mixes omissions with fabrications, counts reordered pages as lost content, and is undefined for unsegmented Chinese. It also measures only the text layer, while REPDF computes OCR word *recall*. Two figures follow. The harness should compute REPDF's metric with Google Cloud Document AI, the engine REPDF names, and a local PaddleOCR proxy in CI; the no-FFI rule permits both in dev tooling. Next to it, report recall, precision (fabricated text), bag-of-words F1 (ordering errors), character F1, and a text-versus-visual gap that flags right-ToUnicode/wrong-glyph outputs.

Whole-page image similarity misses local font errors. In a study of 11 readers, CW-SSIM and pHash were the best render comparators, and even so **an 80% true-positive rate cost a 50% false-positive rate** ([Kuchta et al. 2018](http://srg.doc.ic.ac.uk/files/papers/pdf-emse-18.pdf)). Render-diffs therefore belong on text-box crops with class-aware thresholds. The external oracle battery (qpdf `--check` stdout, the Arlington checker, and at least three renderers) runs in CI, not in the product ([qpdf(1)](https://manpages.debian.org/testing/qpdf/qpdf.1.en.html); [Arlington model](https://github.com/pdf-association/arlington-pdf-model)).

**Splits must group by source document.** With 50 base documents behind all 1,000 files, a random file split leaks the same text and fonts into both tuning and test. Tuning on the scoring set produces bias "of comparable magnitude to differences in performance between learning algorithms" ([Cawley & Talbot 2010](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html)). A 60/20/20 grouped split leaves only about 10 documents in the sealed test. Cluster-bootstrap intervals will therefore be wide, and the CC-MAIN-derived and natural-truncation sets are needed to make claims about generalization.

Since REPDF publishes only aggregates, the defensible claim is **non-inferiority**. It holds when the lower 95% bound of PDFPundit's result, using REPDF's metric in the same stratum, is at least REPDF's point estimate minus a stated margin. Finally, run a **held-out corruptor family** only at release: multi-byte bursts, variable truncation, shifted rather than deleted xrefs, and partial `/Kids` damage. The gap between REPDF-corruptor and held-out recovery is the overfitting indicator that the C9 result above shows is necessary.

## Conclusion

Looking beyond REPDF changes how the problem reads. REPDF's taxonomy looked like a specification to implement. The measurements show it is better treated as one sample of corruption, generated by a tool whose behaviour differs from its own description. The C9 discrepancy is the clearest case. Taking the paper's wording on trust would have produced a ladder that fixes about 3% of damaged streams. Measuring the corpus revealed a cheap, exact method that fixes most of them. The same habit also exposes the font-coverage trap and the ObjStm blind spot. The lesson for PDFPundit's process is to treat every benchmark's generator as data to inspect, and to hold out a corruptor family so that REPDF's quirks never become the product's assumptions.

The second shift is where PDFPundit can lead rather than follow. Nobody has published a fidelity comparison of repair tools. Nobody reports a plausible-but-wrong rate. No engine bounds streams by decompression. No forensic tool uses glyph widths or decipherment to identify fonts that have no names. A pure-Rust tool that uses checksums, grammars and widths as oracles, and that reports what it changed and how confident it is against an independent metric, would advance both the product and the literature. The open risks are font-byte localization cost, how well decipherment copes with Arabic and Han, and whether gains measured in stream bytes carry through to OCR-measured text. All three can be measured in the M7 harness.
