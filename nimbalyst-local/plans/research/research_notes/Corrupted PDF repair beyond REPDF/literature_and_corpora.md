# Corrupted PDF repair beyond REPDF: literature, real-world malformation data, and benchmark corpora

Scope note: research current to 2026-09-26. "Foundational" marks work from before 2015 that is still the main reference for a technique. REPDF = Park, Jeong, Kim, Park, "REPDF: Repairing corrupted PDF files through font mapping and object relationship reconstruction", FSI:DI 56 (2026) 302061, DFRWS EU 2026. C1–C10 are REPDF's corruption classes (C1 header, C2 xref removed, C3 trailer, C4 page tree, C5 object tags stripped, C6 font map lost, C7 FontFile2 deleted, C8 FontFile2+ToUnicode deleted, C9 one byte of a zlib stream changed, C10 tail truncated to about 70%).

## Q1. Which papers cite or compete with REPDF, and what other academic work exists on PDF repair, PDF carving and fragment reassembly? What did each achieve, and how was it measured?

### Takeaway
As of September 2026 no indexed paper cites REPDF. Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex both show 0 citations. Its competition is therefore (a) the commercial tools REPDF itself benchmarked and (b) a small older literature: PDF-specific carving (Cohen 2007, Chen 2008, Al-Sharif 2015/2024, Taşdelen & Süzen 2022), DEFLATE-stream reconstruction (Park et al. 2008, Brown 2011/2013), font-encoding recovery (Vol et al. 2018), and format-agnostic document repair (Docovery 2014). REPDF has almost no advantage on C9 and does worse than two commercial tools on C10 for "Print to PDF" files. Those are the openings PDFPundit can most plausibly exploit.

### Cited Findings
**REPDF itself: status, method of measurement, and where it is weak**
- REPDF appeared in FSI:DI vol. 56, article 302061 (March 2026), as a DFRWS EU 2026 selected paper. It is open access under CC BY-NC-ND. — [DFRWS PDF](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf); [Korea Univ. Pure](https://pure.korea.ac.kr/en/publications/repdf-repairing-corrupted-pdf-files-through-font-mapping-and-obje)
- Citation count as of this research: Semantic Scholar returns `"citationCount": 0, "citations": []`, and OpenAlex returns `cited_by_count: 0`. — [Semantic Scholar API](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.fsidi.2026.302061?fields=title,year,citationCount,citations.title); [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1016/j.fsidi.2026.302061)
- REPDF's metric is word-level exact match. Its formula is "# correctly recovered words / # words in original × 100". Words from both the original and the repaired PDF are extracted with Google Cloud Document AI OCR, and the OCR of the original is the ground truth. The image metric counts (a) images fully and correctly extracted and (b) images correctly rendered in place, measured on 10 files per corruption type. — [REPDF §5.3](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)
- The corpus has 1,000 files: 50 base files × 10 scenarios × 2 creation methods (MS Word "Save As" and "Print to PDF"). There are 6 languages (en, fr, es, ar, hi, zh), and each document has 6 pages with the same content in a different language on each page. The text was generated by GPT-5. — [REPDF §5.2](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)
- Per-class results (REPDF Table 5, text %, Save As / Print to PDF), with baselines iLovePDF / Wondershare Repairit / PDF24:
  - C6: REPDF 88.12 / 88.13 vs iLovePDF 40.90 / 4.87.
  - C7: REPDF 99.51 / 99.21 vs 56.86 / 34.32.
  - C8: REPDF 99.51 / 87.57 vs 41.91 / 4.38.
  - C9: REPDF 58.77 / 62.70 vs iLovePDF 53.09 / 59.68, Wondershare 54.25 / 57.08, PDF24 58.66 / 60.43.
  - C10 (Save As): REPDF 99.58 vs iLovePDF 69.92, Wondershare 0, PDF24 70.53.
  - C10 (Print to PDF): REPDF 30.49 vs iLovePDF 31.47, Wondershare 31.52, PDF24 0.
  - — [REPDF Table 5](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)
- Per-language breakdown (REPDF Table 2):
  - C6 Arabic is 33.46% (Save As) and 39.94% (Print). Dictionary-based font selection fails on Arabic word forms.
  - C10 "Print to PDF" by language: en 60.30, zh 79.68, hi 38.12, es 32.38, fr 1.58, ar 0.05.
  - The authors attribute the C10 Print-to-PDF gap to layout: "'Print to PDF' tends to place text streams and font resources near the end of the file."
  - C9 averages 60.11%.
  - — [REPDF §5.4, Table 2](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)
- REPDF's stated limitations:
  - Each scenario damages a single component, and "it [is] unclear how frequently such damage patterns occur in real-world environments".
  - Only Microsoft Word was used to generate files.
  - Recovery fails if a font is missing from its pre-built font database.
  - Arabic needs an interactive font-selection step.
  - — [REPDF §6](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)
- The REPDF group (Korea University DFRC, corresponding author Jungheum Park) lists REPDF under a "Data Recovery & Reconstruction" research theme. — [DFRC Korea](http://dfrc.korea.ac.kr/DataRecoveryReconstruction)

**PDF-specific carving (mostly foundational)**
- *Chen et al. 2008 (ISISE; foundational):*
  - Method: five validators, namely (1) header/file-length/maximal-object-offset/footer, (2) internal structure (intact-object and fragment-count validation using xref offsets), (3) entropy difference between the last and first 256 bytes of adjacent sectors (threshold 0.8), (4) zlib/deflate trial decompression that slides the sector boundary between two stream halves, and (5) a "character table" of PDF tokens that span a sector boundary (e.g., `/BaseFont /JMODBM+H`).
  - Test data: DFRWS 2007 challenge, which contains 1 intact PDF, 6 two-fragment PDFs, 1 three-fragment PDF and 5 "broken" (missing-piece) PDFs.
  - Results: intact 100%, fragmented 100%, broken fragments 25%. Cohen's carver scored 100% / 85.7% / 0%. Foremost scored 100% intact and 0% otherwise. Revit scored 0%.
  - — [Chen et al. 2008 full text](https://seclab.hdu.edu.cn/static/uploads/paper/08-13.pdf); [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4732193)
- *Cohen 2007, "Advanced carving techniques" (Digital Investigation 4(3–4):119–128; foundational):*
  - Formalizes carving as mapping functions and applies it to PDF and ZIP "based on the internal structure inherent within the file formats themselves".
  - Won the DFRWS 2007 carving challenge with dedicated validators for PDF, ZIP, MIME, HTML and MPEG, and had the lowest false-positive score.
  - — [ACM DL abstract](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.diin.2007.10.001); [DFRWS challenges page](https://dfrws.org/forensic-challenges)
  - Chen 2008 summarizes Cohen's PDF process as: index all objects and xref tables in the image, merge xref tables belonging to the same file, then use them as fixed points to parse and re-parse ambiguous regions. — [Chen 2008](https://seclab.hdu.edu.cn/static/uploads/paper/08-13.pdf)
- *Al-Sharif, Odeh, Al-Saleh 2015 (ITMC; foundational):* carve PDF objects from RAM using the markers at the start and end of each object. PDFs can be carved from RAM "even after closing the PDF file viewer". — [abstract via ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376630317_Measuring_the_Effects_of_Document_Size_on_RAM_Artifacts_A_Memory_Forensics_Approach)
- *Al-Sharif et al. 2024 (FSI:DI 48, 301692):* studies how much of TXT/RTF/DOCX/ODT/PDF content survives in RAM for local, USB and cloud (Dropbox/Google Drive/OneDrive) storage. Results depend significantly on storage medium and document type. "A good ratio" of content is often recovered even after the document is closed and the connection terminated. This paper is one of the 4 "cited by" entries ScienceDirect lists, which include REPDF. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666281724000015)
- *Taşdelen & Süzen 2022 (Tehnički vjesnik 29:1714–1720):* carve Word/PDF from Windows RAM images using header and footer signatures, extract the streams, and insert them into a blank PDF. REPDF notes this cannot repair cases that need Unicode font mappings. — [REPDF §2.2](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)

**Compressed-stream (DEFLATE) reconstruction, which bears directly on C9 and C10 (foundational)**
- *Park, Savoldi, Gubian, J. Park, Lee, Lee 2008 (IJHIT 1(4)):* "Data extraction from damage compressed file". A bit-by-bit scan finds decompressible DEFLATE packets. Brown notes it only recovers fixed-Huffman packets that do not reference unknown earlier bytes. Jungheum Park, REPDF's corresponding author, is a co-author. — [paper](https://gvpress.com/journals/IJHIT/vol1_no4/9.pdf); [Brown 2013 description](https://dfrws.org/sites/default/files/session-files/2013_USA_paper-improved_recovery_and_reconstruction_of_deflated_files.pdf)
- *Brown 2011 (DFRWS USA):*
  - Finds a synchronization point in a DEFLATE bit stream whose beginning is missing or damaged, then decompresses forward into literal bytes plus "co-indexed unknown bytes".
  - Byte trigrams and word unigrams infer the unknown bytes.
  - About 30% of co-indices get unique inferences, and about 75% of unknown bytes are reconstructed with >90% accuracy.
  - Open source.
  - — [DFRWS abstract](https://dfrws.org/presentation/reconstructing-corrupt-deflated-files/)
- *Brown 2013 (DFRWS USA):*
  - Recovers the intact tail of a DEFLATE packet that contains a corrupt section of up to 4–8 KB, and realigns the history window.
  - Compared with 2011, it reconstructs +8.9% absolute more bytes and +20.9% absolute more unknown bytes correctly across 21 Europarl languages, in 1/12 of the time.
  - For corrupted segments of 128–4096 compressed bytes, the output differs from the original by less than twice the uncompressed span of the corruption (mean ratio 1.44–1.65).
  - The reconstruction step applies to any LZ77 codec.
  - Released as ZipRec 1.0 (GPL).
  - — [paper](https://dfrws.org/sites/default/files/session-files/2013_USA_paper-improved_recovery_and_reconstruction_of_deflated_files.pdf)

**Font and Unicode recovery (bears on C6–C8)**
- *Vol, Krutko, Stefanovitch, Postanogov 2018 (IAPR DAS):* a CNN classifies rendered glyphs into homoglyph groups, and a language model derives the glyph-to-character mapping for PDFs that render correctly but extract garbage. Semantic Scholar lists 3 citations, one of which is REPDF. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Automatic-Recovery-of-Corrupted-Font-Encoding-in-Vol-Krutko/e0c6171f58eef6f5b7ef5b388d8c56868e8f5519); [REPDF §2.2](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REPDF-Repairing-corrupted-PDF-files-through-f_2026_Forensic-Science-Interna.pdf)
- Digital-preservation data on font coverage: Woods & Brown found "up to 79%" of documents from a wide range of sources render accurately with fonts in a modern Windows+Office desktop, rising to 92% with family-name matching and foundry data. Van der Knijff's Preflight run on GovDocs found "the majority of failed tests is font-related". — [DPC PDF Format Preservation Assessment 2015](https://wiki.dpconline.org/images/5/51/PDF_Assessment_v1.2_external.pdf)
- Bast & Korzen 2017, "A Benchmark and Evaluation for Text Extraction from PDF" (JCDL), is a ground-truth text benchmark for PDF extraction tools. — [paper](https://ad-publications.cs.uni-freiburg.de/benchmark.pdf); [GitHub](https://github.com/ckorzen/pdf-text-extraction-benchmark)

**Format-agnostic document repair and PDF correctness studies**
- *Docovery (Kuchta, Cadar, Castro, Costa, ASE 2014):* uses symbolic execution (KLEE) of the reader program. Starting from the failing path, it explores alternative paths and makes small byte changes so the document loads while staying similar to the original. It needs no format knowledge. The case studies were pine, pr, dwarfdump and readelf, not PDF. The authors list open challenges: "complex structured formats, supporting the addition and removal of bytes, and scaling to larger documents". It also cites SOAP/automatic input rectification (Long et al. ICSE 2012) and data-structure repair (Demsky & Rinard). — [paper](http://srg.doc.ic.ac.uk/files/papers/docovery-ase-14.pdf)
- *Kuchta, Lutellier, Wong, Tan, Cadar 2018 (Empirical Software Engineering 23:3187–3220):*
  - Measured on 230K GovDocs1 PDFs with 11 readers.
  - 13.5% of PDFs render inconsistently in at least one popular reader, which projects to 28K–33K inconsistent files in the corpus.
  - Found 30 unique bugs and reported 33.
  - GovDocs1 version mix: 1.4 = 37.6%, 1.3 = 22.8%, 1.2 = 17.8%, 1.5 = 9.6%, 1.6 = 8.3%.
  - The authors suggest document-recovery techniques can use cross-reader inconsistency detection and image similarity to judge repair quality.
  - — [paper PDF](http://srg.doc.ic.ac.uk/files/papers/pdf-emse-18.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323665101_On_the_correctness_of_electronic_documents_studying_finding_and_localizing_inconsistency_bugs_in_PDF_readers_and_files)
- *Lam, Li, Anderson 2024 (IEEE TIFS 19:7774–7782), VALARIN:* builds error-feature vectors from many PDF parsers plus an OWL ontology. "PDF parsers exhibit mutual biases when recovering from specification ambiguities", and parser consensus on error features tracks file safety. REPDF cites it. — [ACM DL abstract](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TIFS.2024.3445733)

**Adjacent and recent (2023–2026) work**
- *Boiko 2023:* a syntactic method for reconstructing highly fragmented OOXML (ZIP/DEFLATE) files. Its effectiveness "depends mainly on data verification methods during the reconstruction of local elements that occupy at least three clusters". — [Radioelectronic and Computer Systems](https://nti.khai.edu/ojs/index.php/reks/article/view/reks.2023.1.14)
- *Laban et al., April 2026 (arXiv 2604.15597):* LLMs delegated to edit structured documents "introduce sparse but severe errors that silently corrupt documents". This is a caution for REPDF's proposed "AI-based contextual" font selection. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15597)

### Inferences
- REPDF is too new (March 2026) to have follow-ups. The realistic competitors for PDFPundit's paper are REPDF's own baselines plus the older carving and DEFLATE literature. A rigorous comparison should re-run REPDF's public corpus with REPDF's own metric.
- **C9 is REPDF's weakest point.** It gets 58.8–62.7% against 53–60% for commercial tools, so it adds about 0–5 points. Brown's DEFLATE resynchronization plus n-gram reconstruction (2011/2013) directly addresses mid-stream corruption, and REPDF does not cite it. For a single changed byte there is also a cheap exhaustive option: try up to 255 values × n positions, start decoding at the damaged block, and accept a candidate only if it decodes to valid content-stream syntax and the Adler-32 matches. This suggests C9 could approach C1–C5 levels. That is an inference and needs verification.
- **C10 "Print to PDF" is REPDF's second weak point** (30.49%, below two commercial tools). Recovering it probably requires salvaging partial streams, i.e. inflating a truncated Flate stream up to the cut, and recovering content from any linearized first-page section. It cannot rely on whole objects.
- REPDF's OCR-on-both-sides metric hides extraction-layer errors: text that renders correctly but has a wrong ToUnicode still scores 100%. PDFPundit could report both the OCR metric and a text-layer metric (extracted Unicode vs ground truth), for example in the style of Bast & Korzen, so its results are comparable with REPDF and also stricter.
- Vol et al. (glyph CNN plus language model) complements REPDF's font-database approach. It does not need the font to be in a database, which addresses REPDF's stated failure mode and the "Print to PDF" obfuscated font names such as `CIDFont+F1`.

- Google Scholar "cited by" could not be queried (not accessible from this environment). The citation check relies on Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex only; both showed 0 citations.
- No quantitative results were retrieved for Vol et al. 2018 (accuracy), Taşdelen & Süzen 2022, Al-Sharif 2015, or Lam et al. 2024 (paywalled).

## Q2. What did the DARPA SafeDocs programme and the LangSec community find about how real-world PDFs are malformed and how parsers silently repair them? Which findings bear on a carver's heuristics?

### Takeaway
At web scale, malformation and silent repair are normal:
- 25% of about 8M Common Crawl PDFs raise at least one Arlington grammar error.
- iText had to rebuild the xref for 3.1% of them.
- 44% use xref streams and 23% use hybrid xref.
- A strict parser accepted only 14.65% of 10,000 web PDFs (Caradoc).
- Major readers repair differently from each other and outside the spec, e.g. PDFium expands object streams proactively while pdf.js expands them lazily.

For a carver, xref streams and object streams matter most. Objects packed inside an ObjStm have no `obj`/`endobj` markers, so a scanner built only on REPDF-style tag scanning misses them.

### Cited Findings
**Programme and tools**
- DARPA SafeDocs aimed to define a verification-friendly subset of document formats, plus parser construction kits and translators. Its public outputs include the Arlington PDF Model, the 8M-PDF Digital Corpora corpus, SPARCLUR (wrappers for comparing extant parsers), DaeDaLus, Hammer, Parsley, File Observatory, Format Analysis Workbench (FAW), Dowker statistics tools, and PolyFile. — [DARPA SafeDocs](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/safe-documents)

**Arlington PDF Model (Wyatt, LangSec 2021)**
- A machine-readable model of PDF 2.0: 514 objects, 3,551 keys and array elements, 40 custom predicates encoding over 5,000 rules. It "highlighted various extant data malformations and triggered multiple changes to the PDF 2.0 specification". — [PDF Association](https://pdfa.org/safedocs-latest-research-into-securing-pdf/)
- Malformations found in the wild include misspelt or mis-capitalized keys: `/type` vs `/Type`, `/SubType` vs `/Subtype`, `/XObjcect`, `/Blackls1` vs `/BlackIs1`. — [Wyatt LangSec 2021](https://github.com/gangtan/LangSec-papers-and-slides/raw/main/langsec21/papers/Wyatt_LangSec21.pdf)

**CC-MAIN-2021-31 File Observatory statistics (Allison, JPL, PDF Days 2022)**
- Corpus: 8.3M PDFs. 6.4M came from Common Crawl (1.6 TB) and 1.9M were refetched (9.8 TB), with about 100k refetch failures. 7.9M unique hashes, 10 TB stored. — [Allison 2022 slides](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)
- iText structural flags: Rebuilt XREF 242K (3.1%), Fixed XREF 278 (0.004%), Hybrid XREF 1.8M (23%), XREF Stream 3.5M (44%), Encrypted 167K (2.1%). pdfinfo flags: Linearized 2.2M (28%), Tagged 2.6M (34%). — [Allison 2022](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)
- Arlington grammar checker over the corpus: "At least one warning: 16%. At least one error: 25%". Self-reported header version often disagrees with the latest feature version used (confusion matrix). — [Allison 2022](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)
- Apache Tika (PDFBox) exit values: 7,915,273 OK, 15,523 caught exceptions ("malformed PDFs" among them), 129 timeouts, 11 OOM. — [Allison 2022](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)
- Version mix: 1.4 = 27.3%, 1.7 = 26.6%, 1.5 = 22.7%, 1.6 = 12.1%, 1.3 = 9.7%, 2.0 = 0.03%.
- Top producers: Adobe PDF Library 15.0 (598,598), MS Word 2016 (328,678), MS Word 2010 (248,089), and "Microsoft: Print To PDF" (153,083).
- — [Allison 2022](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)
- Issue-tracker ("stressful") corpus vs control: 519 parse exceptions (1.6%) vs 16 (0.05%), described as "a highly condensed set of stressful files". — [Allison et al. LangSec 2021](https://github.com/gangtan/LangSec-papers-and-slides/raw/main/langsec21/papers/Allison_LangSec21.pdf)

**Strict-parsing and multi-parser studies**
- *Caradoc (Endignoux, Levillain, Migeon; LangSec 2016; foundational):*
  - Of 10,000 files from random web-search queries, the strict parser accepted 1,465 (14.65%), and 536 passed type checking.
  - After normalization 8,993 files were processed, of which 1,429 type-checked and 1,391 had type errors.
  - Its type checker caught typos such as `/Blackls1`.
  - The slides illustrate the incremental-update structure: an appended xref section, trailer with `/Prev`, and multiple `%%EOF`.
  - — [Caradoc slides](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/6ccb/56a393aa5540be5669e81295fa5fe30a814f.pdf); [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7527763)
- *Robinson 2020 (SafeDocs):*
  - Data: 10,000 files from the SafeDocs "Evaluation 2" test and evaluation team, split into `internet_sourced` and `dangerous` sets with manual valid/rejected ground truth.
  - Method: stderr from many parsers (caradoc, hammer, pdfium, xpdf, …) was matched with 955 regexes, and a Bernoulli pseudo-likelihood test identified non-compliant files format-agnostically.
  - Parsers often emit repeated messages "if the parser attempts to repair a slightly non-compliant file as it proceeds".
  - "There is not much redundancy present in the behaviors of parsers for the PDF specification."
  - — [arXiv 2012.10211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10211)
- Kuchta et al. 2018: 13.5% of real PDFs render inconsistently across popular readers, with causes in both the readers and the files. — [EMSE 2018](http://srg.doc.ic.ac.uk/files/papers/pdf-emse-18.pdf)

**How specific parsers silently repair**
- *MuPDF:* "Upon a broken cross reference table or other parse errors MuPDF will restart parsing the file from the beginning to try to rebuild a (hopefully correct) cross reference table." `pdf_check_document` is described as "very expensive". A repaired document cannot be saved incrementally. — [mupdf document.h](https://github.com/ArtifexSoftware/mupdf/blob/master/include/mupdf/pdf/document.h)
- *PDFium vs pdf.js vs lopdf:*
  - PDFium `RebuildCrossRef()` scans the whole file for `N G obj`. For each object found it immediately tries to parse it as an object stream, and on success adds all contained objects to the xref.
  - pdf.js expands object streams only on demand, so it fails if the ObjStm itself was not discovered.
  - A test file whose object is marked free in the xref but exists in the file opens in PDFium, fails in pdf.js and Adobe Reader, and lopdf parses 0 pages.
  - The issue reports production PDFs (medical records) that open in Firefox and Chrome but give 0 pages in lopdf.
  - — [lopdf issue #449 (Nov 2025)](https://github.com/J-F-Liu/lopdf/issues/449)
- *qpdf:*
  - The maintainer says "repairing a PDF file requires guesswork no matter how you do it". qpdf's repair "is based partially on appendices from early versions of the PDF spec plus years of seeing broken files". Different tools will each repair files the others cannot. — [qpdf #1140](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/issues/1140)
  - A real file (Neevia docuPrinter) had `startxref` off by one byte. qpdf was relaxed to look for `xref` near the stated offset, because "this kind of auto-recover technique is almost always worth it". — [qpdf #341](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/issues/341)
  - "Recovering broken files is pretty hard -- it's all heuristics, and it's easy for a change to break previous successful recoveries." — [qpdf #1103](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/issues/1103)
- *PDF Association errata discussion (2025):*
  - "Most implementations also accept PDFs with cross reference tables in the initial revision that don't have the 0 entry, or that are arbitrarily segmented with gaps or even overlaps."
  - Chromium's viewer handles gapped subsections but has problems with a single subsection that lists free entries explicitly.
  - Peter Wyatt: "what they recover vs what other implementations recover may well differ since it is outside the spec".
  - — [pdf-issues #517](https://github.com/pdf-association/pdf-issues/issues/517)
- *Object streams (qpdf docs):* objects in object streams always have generation 0. The ObjStm body is N pairs of (object number, offset relative to `/First`) followed by concatenated objects. Acrobat 6 puts no more than 100 objects per ObjStm for linearized files and 200 for non-linearized files, and qpdf writes at most 100. For hybrid files the trailer's `/XRefStm` points to a supplementary xref stream. — [qpdf object streams](https://qpdf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/object-streams.html)

### Inferences
- **Object streams are the largest blind spot for obj/endobj scanning.** About 44% of web PDFs use xref streams, and those generally put most non-stream objects inside ObjStms, including page dictionaries, font dictionaries and often the catalog. A C2 or C5 repair that only scans for `N G obj … endobj` finds the ObjStm container but not its members unless it proactively inflates every candidate stream and parses the `/N`/`/First` header, as PDFium does. REPDF generated all its files with Word, whose object-stream use is not reported in the paper, so its C2/C5 100% may not transfer to ObjStm-heavy producers. Because lopdf does not recover these objects (issue #449), PDFPundit's own scanner must.
- **Stale xref offsets are still useful evidence.** qpdf's off-by-one fix and pdf-issues #517 show that real xrefs are often slightly wrong, gapped or overlapping. A carver should treat xref and `startxref` values as noisy constraints, e.g. "search ±k bytes", not as ground truth or as garbage. Cohen's 2007 mapping-function approach used the same idea for fragment reassembly.
- **Key typos and case errors** (`/type`, `/SubType`) should be normalized case-insensitively when classifying objects during a scan: page vs font vs XObject.
- **Parser differentials can serve as a repair oracle.** Robinson and Lam show that the pattern of error messages across several parsers classifies files well. A repaired output could be scored by opening it in several independent parsers (e.g., pdf.js/PDFium/MuPDF/qpdf) and minimizing warnings or disagreement. Kuchta 2018 suggests image similarity across readers as a complementary check.
- About 25% of web PDFs are "malformed" by Arlington and 3.1% need an xref rebuild. A carver's validity checks must therefore be lenient (Postel-style). A strict validator (Caradoc accepted 14.65%) would reject most real files, including undamaged ones.

### Gaps
- No primary SafeDocs paper titled "PDF is a mess" was found. The closest quantitative sources are the Allison 2020/2021/2022 File Observatory work, Caradoc, Robinson, and Kuchta.
- The Allison slides give no breakdown of the 25% Arlington errors or the 3.1% iText rebuilds by error type (e.g. wrong offsets vs missing xref vs truncation).
- Results from the Galois Format Analysis Workbench and SPARCLUR parser-differential studies were not retrieved.

## Q3. What real-world PDF corruption distributions are documented (truncated downloads, bad incremental updates, broken xrefs from editors, transfer-mode/CRLF damage), how do they compare with REPDF's synthetic C1–C10, and is REPDF's taxonomy missing common real-world classes?

### Takeaway
No study publishes a census of the causes of damage in user-reported corrupted PDFs; this is a real gap in the literature. The best proxies are:
- Web-crawl truncation: 22% of Common Crawl PDFs in Dec 2019, and about 1.9M of 8.3M in 2021 needed refetching.
- Parser xref rebuilds: 3.1%.
- Cross-reader inconsistency: 13.5% of GovDocs1.
- Grammar errors: 25%.
- Evidence of editor-produced bad offsets and ASCII-mode transfer damage.

REPDF's C1–C10 are single-fault, Word-only inductions. They omit classes that the prevalence data shows matter: object and xref stream damage, hybrid, linearized and incremental-update chains, CR/LF transfer damage, mid-file sector loss and fragmentation, loss of the file head, wrong `/Length`, non-Flate filter damage, non-TrueType fonts, and encryption dictionaries.

### Cited Findings
**Truncation (C10)**
- Common Crawl truncates at 1 MB. In the December 2019 crawl "nearly 430,000 PDFs (22%) were truncated". — [Allison et al. LangSec 2020](http://spw20.langsec.org/papers/corpus_LangSec2020.pdf); [pdf-corpora index](https://github.com/pdf-association/pdf-corpora)
- CC-MAIN-2021-31: "roughly 6 million files directly from Common Crawl" and "nearly 2 million" that Common Crawl had flagged as truncated, refetched from the original URLs. Common Crawl keeps a per-record truncation flag. — [Constructing the corpus](https://digitalcorpora.org/corpora/file-corpora/cc-main-2021-31-pdf-untruncated/constructing-the-cc-main-2021-31-pdf-untruncated-corpus/); [Allison 2022](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)
- Vendor guidance lists the causes of corrupted PDFs as incomplete downloads, storage errors, transfer errors, malware, and crashes or power loss during save. Low-weight source: vendor blog, no data. — [Smallpdf](https://smallpdf.com/blog/repair-pdf-file)

**Broken or incorrect xref (C2/C3-like)**
- 3.1% (242K) of CC-MAIN-2021-31 PDFs needed an iText xref rebuild. — [Allison 2022](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)
- A producer-caused example: in a Companies House PDF "the offset of the cross reference table and most object offsets in it are completely wrong". The first offsets are correct up to the first image stream, so the producer apparently did not count image-stream bytes, or swapped in larger images without updating offsets. — [Stack Overflow answer by mkl (PDF expert)](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/46485766/could-not-find-x-ref-table-pdf)
- Another producer-caused example: `startxref` off by one byte in a Neevia docuPrinter PDF. — [qpdf #341](https://github.com/qpdf/qpdf/issues/341)
- JHOVE PDF-HUL-38 ("Invalid object definition") case study: a conference paper PDF with broken objects. qpdf "pointed us to several broken objects". The file also had two different version entries. — [OPF blog, Lindlar 2022](https://openpreservation.org/blogs/trouble-shooting-pdf-validation-errors-a-case-of-pdf-hul-38)

**Transfer-mode / CR-LF damage (not in C1–C10)**
- ISO 32000 says that if a PDF contains binary data, the header must be followed by a comment line with at least four bytes ≥128. The purpose is that transfer programs treat the file as binary. — [PDF Association errata, ISO 32000-2 cl. 7](https://pdf-issues.pdfa.org/32000-2-2020/clause07.html); [pdf-issues #272](https://github.com/pdf-association/pdf-issues/issues/272)
- gzip FAQ: "99.9% of the problems with gzip are due to file transfers done in ASCII mode instead of BINARY mode". `fixgz` removes each CR that precedes an LF and comes with "absolutely no guarantee". This is a DEFLATE-data precedent for undoing transfer damage. — [gzip.org](https://www.gzip.org/ancient/); [fixgz.c](https://www.gzip.org/ancient/fixgz.c)
- The reverse direction also happens: a 7z archive uploaded in ASCII mode lost "all hex 0D characters". — [7-Zip forum](https://sourceforge.net/p/sevenzip/discussion/45797/thread/7e8c5608/)

**Incremental updates (not in C1–C10)**
- Incremental updates append a new xref section and trailer with `/Prev` and a second `%%EOF`. — [Caradoc slides](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/6ccb/56a393aa5540be5669e81295fa5fe30a814f.pdf)
- Signature attacks use malformed incremental updates (Mladenov et al., "Incremental Saving Attack") and well-formed ones (Mainka et al., "shadow attacks"). Source: secondary summary. — [arXiv 2507.00827 summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00827)
- MuPDF will not save a repaired document incrementally. — [mupdf document.h](https://github.com/ArtifexSoftware/mupdf/blob/master/include/mupdf/pdf/document.h)

**Prevalence of structures that REPDF's inductions do not exercise**
- Xref streams 44%, hybrid xref 23%, linearized 28%, encrypted about 2% of web PDFs. — [Allison 2022](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)
- Common filters beyond Flate include DCTDecode, JPXDecode, CCITTFaxDecode, JBIG2Decode, LZWDecode, RunLength and ASCIIHex/ASCII85. Changing line endings in bulk corrupts binary stream data. Secondary source. — [losLab overview](https://blog.loslab.com/en/pdf-structure/understanding-pdf-file-structure-a-technical-overview)
- The top producers are Word (Save As) and "Microsoft: Print To PDF" (153K files), so REPDF's two creation paths are realistic. Adobe PDF Library (598K) and many others are absent from its corpus. — [Allison 2022](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1415-Allison.pdf)

**Cross-reader and preservation evidence**
- 13.5% of GovDocs1 PDFs render inconsistently in at least one reader. — [Kuchta 2018](http://srg.doc.ic.ac.uk/files/papers/pdf-emse-18.pdf)
- The DPC 2015 assessment says invalid or badly formed PDFs in deposited collections are "highly likely", but "the severity and frequency of the risks … remain relatively poorly understood". — [DPC](https://wiki.dpconline.org/images/5/51/PDF_Assessment_v1.2_external.pdf)

**Fragmentation on disk (relevant to carving damaged PDFs)**
- Garfinkel 2007 (foundational): "Overall 125,659 (6%) of the files we recovered from the corpus were fragmented", from more than 300 second-hand drives (324 with more than five files). Most fragmented files were bifragmented. The most common gap was 8 blocks (4,327 files). Forensically interesting types were "significantly" more fragmented. — [Garfinkel 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1742287607000369)
- The NTFS subset of Garfinkel's data derives to 12.2%. — [van der Meer et al. 2021](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2021_APAC_paper-a_contemporary_investigation_of_ntfs_file_fragmentation.pdf)
- van der Meer et al. 2021 (220 in-use Windows laptops, Oct 2018–Jan 2019): out-of-order fragmentation is common, e.g. 44.1% of fragmented BMP, 38.0% of PNG and 37.6% of RAW files. "A significant group of fragmented files for which reconstruction is insufficiently addressed by current tooling." — [van der Meer et al. 2021](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2021_APAC_paper-a_contemporary_investigation_of_ntfs_file_fragmentation.pdf)

### Inferences
**Mapping REPDF's classes to real-world evidence:**
- C1 (header overwrite) matches transfer and filesystem damage at offset 0. However, real files also show garbage *before* `%PDF` (junk prefixes, polyglots), which REPDF does not induce.
- C2 and C3 are realistic but idealized. Real damage is more often *wrong* offsets than a *removed* table: producer bugs, editors that append without updating, and off-by-one errors. The 3.1% iText rebuild rate is the best proxy.
- C10 matches download and crawl truncation, which the 22% Common Crawl figure shows is very common. Real truncation points follow an unknown size distribution (e.g., the 1 MB crawl cap), not a fixed "keep 70%".
- C4–C8 are, as REPDF admits, of unknown real-world frequency. They look more like tool bugs or deliberate stripping than accidental damage.
- C9's single random byte is a plausible bit-rot model. Real storage and transfer damage is more often multi-byte or sector-sized (zeroed or overwritten 512 B/4 KB blocks), or systematic (CR/LF insertion or removal throughout the file).

**Classes missing from REPDF's taxonomy that PDFPundit could add (each is an inference grounded in the prevalence data above):**
- (a) Damage to an ObjStm or xref stream, which loses dozens to hundreds of objects at once (44% xref-stream prevalence; up to 100–200 objects per ObjStm).
- (b) Broken `/Prev` or incremental-update chains, including a truncated last revision, where earlier revisions stay recoverable.
- (c) Linearized files (28%) truncated after the first-page section, and a damaged hint stream or linearization dictionary.
- (d) CR/LF transfer damage: global LF→CRLF or CRLF→LF, which shifts every offset and corrupts binary streams.
- (e) Sector-level holes: zeroed or overwritten blocks mid-file.
- (f) Fragmented or out-of-order carved files.
- (g) Loss of the head of the file: missing the first N KB, including the catalog and possibly the first ObjStm.
- (h) Wrong or missing stream `/Length`.
- (i) Damage in non-Flate filters and in Flate with PNG predictors, as used in xref streams.
- (j) Fonts other than FontFile2: Type1/FontFile, CFF/FontFile3, Type3, and non-embedded standard-14 fonts. REPDF's C7/C8 only model FontFile2.
- (k) Encrypted files (about 2%) whose `/Encrypt` or `/ID` is damaged.
- (l) Multi-fault combinations. REPDF induces one fault per file.

### Gaps
- No published breakdown of the *causes* of damage for PDFs that users report as corrupted, e.g. from Adobe support data, repair-tool telemetry or a forensic casework study, was found.
- Garfinkel's and van der Meer's PDF-specific fragmentation rates (per-extension tables) were not retrieved. Only the overall rates and image-type figures are cited above.
- No quantitative prevalence of CR/LF-damaged, incrementally-updated-then-truncated, or wrong-`/Length` PDFs in the wild was found.

## Q4. Which public corpora of corrupted or malformed PDFs exist? What are their size and licensing, and do they include ground-truth originals?

- A defensible PDFPundit benchmark could combine:

## Q5. Is there work on file-type-agnostic carving of compressed or object-structured formats (e.g., bifragment gap carving, graph-theoretic fragment reassembly) that transfers to PDF?

### Takeaway
Yes. The most transferable ideas are:
1. Garfinkel's fast object validation and bifragment gap carving (foundational 2007), including his "known offset" variant that uses a self-referential pointer, the MSOLE analogue of a PDF xref offset.
2. Cohen's mapping-function carving, which was already applied to PDF (2007).
3. Pal/Memon graph-theoretic reassembly and sequential-hypothesis fragmentation-point detection.
4. Brown's DEFLATE resynchronization and LM reconstruction, which recovers data from mid-stream and headless Flate fragments.
5. Format-agnostic validity oracles: Docovery-style program analysis and Robinson/Lam multi-parser error statistics.

Byte-level fragment-type classifiers (FiFTy etc.) reach only about 71–84% accuracy on generic types and are weak for container formats like PDF.

### Cited Findings
- *Garfinkel 2007 (foundational):*
  - Frames carving as "a multi-tier decision problem that seeks to quickly validate or discard candidate byte strings – 'objects'", with validators for JPEG, MSOLE and ZIP.
  - Bifragment Gap Carving: known header sector s1 and footer e2; search for e1 and s2.
  - For MSOLE, "Bifragment Carving with constant size and known offset" uses the header's pointer to the MSAT, which must lie at a computable offset in the second fragment. This is O(n³) if the header location is known. It recovered all bifragmented Word/Excel files in the DFRWS 2006 challenge, though with some false positives that opened but had wrong data.
  - For formats without a footer: trim a byte at a time until validation fails.
  - — [Garfinkel 2007](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1742287607000369)
- *Pal & Memon 2006 (IEEE TIP; foundational):* image reassembly formulated as a k-vertex-disjoint path problem, with greedy heuristics (including Parallel Unique Path). "Images can be reconstructed with high accuracy even when there are thousands of fragments". — [ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/scientific-contributions/Anandabrata-Pal-28359484)
- *Pal, Sencar, Memon 2008 (DFRWS best paper; foundational):* sequential hypothesis testing compares adjacent block pairs to find a fragmentation point, evaluated on the DFRWS 2006/2007 sets. SmartCarving "was able to recover all but one fragmented JPEG" in those challenges. — [DFRWS](https://dfrws.org/presentation/detecting-file-fragmentation-point-using-sequential-hypothesis-testing); [Forensics Wiki](https://forensics.wiki/file_carving_smartcarving)
- *Cohen 2007:* formal carving theory applied to PDF and ZIP internal structure. It had the lowest false positives in DFRWS 2007. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.diin.2007.10.001); [DFRWS](https://dfrws.org/forensic-challenges)
- *Chen 2008:* the PDF-specific validators listed in Q1 (entropy difference at sector seams, trial inflate across candidate seams, token-table matching at seams) achieved 100% on the DFRWS 2007 fragmented PDFs. — [Chen 2008](https://seclab.hdu.edu.cn/static/uploads/paper/08-13.pdf)
- *Brown 2011/2013 and Park et al. 2008:* recover and reconstruct DEFLATE data when the stream start or a middle section is missing (numbers in Q1). "The reconstruction algorithm is applicable with minimal adaptation to any LZ77-based compression algorithm." — [Brown 2013](https://dfrws.org/sites/default/files/session-files/2013_USA_paper-improved_recovery_and_reconstruction_of_deflated_files.pdf)
- *Gladyshev & James 2017:* decision-theoretic file carving, which trades completeness for time. — [cited in ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319294817_Decision-theoretic_file_carving)
- *Boiko 2023:* syntactic reassembly of fragmented OOXML (ZIP+DEFLATE+XML), independent of content. — [paper page](https://nti.khai.edu/ojs/index.php/reks/article/view/reks.2023.1.14)
- *Fragment classification:*
  - FiFTy (Mittal, Korus, Memon; IEEE TIFS 2021): 75 file types, average accuracy 77.5% at about 38 s/GB. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.06148v1)
  - ByteRCNN on the FiFTy data: 71.1% (512 B) and 83.9% (4 KB). — [ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/342370999_FiFTy_Large-Scale_File_Fragment_Type_Identification_Using_Convolutional_Neural_Networks)
  - Roussev & Garfinkel 2009 argue "specialized methods targeted to each specific file type will be necessary" for fragment classification. — [Semantic Scholar listing](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Hierarchy-Based-File-Fragment-Classification-Bhatt-Mishra/eb4e542f5b71a4d4ec1858be30ad26bfe5b12eb9)
- *Format-agnostic repair oracles:* Docovery (symbolic execution, no format knowledge; see Q1) and Robinson 2020 (multi-parser error-message statistics). — [Docovery](http://srg.doc.ic.ac.uk/files/papers/docovery-ase-14.pdf); [Robinson](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10211)
- *Out-of-order fragmentation* is common on modern NTFS (38–44% of fragmented image files). — [van der Meer 2021](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/2021_APAC_paper-a_contemporary_investigation_of_ntfs_file_fragmentation.pdf)

### Inferences
**How these ideas transfer to PDF:**
- A PDF gives Garfinkel/Cohen-style carvers unusually strong self-describing constraints:
  - (a) Every xref entry, and every ObjStm offset table, is a claim that "object N starts at byte X". A candidate fragment ordering can be scored by how many `N G obj` headers land exactly on claimed offsets. This is the PDF analogue of Garfinkel's "known offset" MSOLE carving and Cohen's mapping function.
  - (b) `/Length` gives stream extents.
  - (c) `/Size`, `/Count` and `/Kids` give global counts.
  - (d) Flate streams carry an Adler-32 checksum that validates a candidate seam with near-zero false positives. Chen's trial-inflate validator, extended with Adler-32 checking, is stronger than entropy.
- For C9 and mid-file holes, Brown's resynchronization plus a PDF-specific language model (content-stream operators like `BT … Tf … Tj … ET`, number/operator grammar, and ToUnicode-decoded text n-grams) should reconstruct more than Europarl-style text models. PDF content streams are highly regular, so this is a promising extension that has not been published.
- Fragment-type classifiers are weak for PDF because a PDF block may be JPEG, Flate or plain text. PDF-aware validators (token tables, inflate probes, object-header regexes) are more reliable than generic ML classifiers for deciding "is this block PDF?". That matches Roussev & Garfinkel's argument.
- Because out-of-order fragments are common, PDFPundit's reassembly should not assume increasing disk order. Graph formulations (Pal/Memon k-disjoint paths), with edge weights from xref-offset consistency and inflate/Adler validation, fit well.

### Gaps
- No published work applying Pal/Memon-style graph reassembly or sequential hypothesis testing specifically to PDF was found. Chen 2008 and Cohen 2007 handle only 2–3 fragments on the DFRWS 2007 set.
- No evaluation of Brown's ZipRec on PDF Flate streams, or of language models trained on PDF content-stream syntax, was found.
- FiFTy's per-class accuracy for PDF fragments was not retrieved.
