# Font and text recovery for C6–C8: glyph-code → Unicode recovery and font identification

Scope: recovering correct text when a PDF's font information is damaged: lost `/ToUnicode`, deleted embedded font programs (`/FontFile2`), deleted font resource dictionaries. PDFPundit's plan was read first (technical design §5, §18, §19, §20; desktop-app plan "Prior Art" and "Risks"). Research current to September 2026. Foundational work is marked with its year.

Note on the taxonomy, because it changes what "C6" means. In PDFPundit's own plan, C6 = "Deletes `/Font` in a page's `/Resources`" and the fix is "Re-link `/Page` → `/Font` from carved font objects". The detector is "a `/Page` `/Resources` lacks `/Font` while its content stream uses `Tf`". C7 = `/FontFile2` removed. C8 = `/FontFile2` **and** `/ToUnicode` removed. So in C6 the font objects themselves (ToUnicode, FontFile, `/W`) usually survive as carvable orphans, and in C8 the `/W`/`/Widths` arrays, `/FontDescriptor`, `/BaseFont`, `/Encoding` and `CIDSystemInfo` survive. Sources: `/home/user/PDFPundit/nimbalyst-local/plans/pdfpundit-desktop-app.md` (taxonomy table) and `/home/user/PDFPundit/nimbalyst-local/plans/pdfpundit-technical-design.md` §19. The brief's phrase "C6 (ToUnicode/font map lost)" is looser than the plan's definition. The notes below cover both readings.

## 1. Decipherment framing: can language-model decipherment replace or back up dictionary scoring, especially when the document's font is not in the bundled database?

### Takeaway
Treating unknown glyph codes as a 1:1 (or homophonic) substitution cipher solved with a character n-gram or neural language model is a mature, well-benchmarked technique. For alphabetic text it reaches near-zero symbol error once there are about 64–128 characters per font, and ~5% at 32 characters. It needs no font database at all, so it is the right backup (and sometimes the primary method) when the document's font is not bundled or when glyph IDs were renumbered. It is weak on very short runs (<~30 characters, the unicity distance). There is no published accuracy for large-alphabet CJK. Zero-shot general LLMs are not reliable solvers of arbitrary-key substitution. A 2026 forensic system (CPR) used an 8B LLM only as a yes/no validator, and even then recovered only 33% of files when both ToUnicode and FontFile were deleted.

### Cited Findings
- **Shannon unicity distance (1949, foundational):** a simple substitution on English has ~88.4 bits of key entropy (log2 26!). English redundancy is ~3.2 bits/char, so about 28 characters of ciphertext are the theoretical minimum for a unique solution — [Wikipedia: Unicity distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicity_distance); Shannon's original gives "about 30 letters" — [Shannon 1949, Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~rist/642-spring-2014/shannon-secrecy.pdf).
- In practice "you may need 4 times more characters (100 or more) to reliably break substitution ciphers" — [Practical Cryptography: Unicity Distance](http://www.practicalcryptography.com/cryptanalysis/text-characterisation/statistics/); "typically about 50 letters are needed" — [Wikipedia: Substitution cipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_cipher). A homophonic cipher with N=30 symbols has a unicity distance of ~38 characters — [Practical Cryptography](http://www.practicalcryptography.com/cryptanalysis/text-characterisation/statistics/).
- **Ravi & Knight (EMNLP 2008):** exact (integer-programming) decipherment with low-order n-gram LMs. It shows how accuracy varies with cipher length and n-gram order — [ACL Anthology D08-1085](https://aclanthology.org/D08-1085). A follow-up (Ravi & Knight 2011, Bayesian) found that trigram models plus a word list gave the best results — [HistoCrypt 2023 survey](https://ecp.ep.liu.se/index.php/histocrypt/article/download/701/607/718).
- **Nuhn, Schamper & Ney (ACL 2013), beam search:** on 1:1 substitution ciphers the guaranteed-optimal 3-gram solution has **38.6%** decipherment error. Beam search with a **6-gram** LM gets **4.13%** "in a fraction of time" — [ACL Anthology P13-1154](https://aclanthology.org/P13-1154). Higher-order models do better (6-grams best in Nuhn et al. 2014) — [HistoCrypt 2023](https://ecp.ep.liu.se/index.php/histocrypt/article/download/701/607/718).
- **Hauer, Hayward & Kondrak (COLING 2014):** combining **character-level and word-level** LMs specifically for *short* monoalphabetic ciphers, using Monte Carlo Tree Search as a fast alternative to beam search — [ACL Anthology C14-1218](https://aclanthology.org/C14-1218.pdf).
- **Kambhatla, Mansouri Bigvand & Sarkar (EMNLP 2018), neural-LM beam search:** symbol error rate (SER) by cipher length 16/32/64/128/256 = **26.8% / 5.8% / 0.07% / 0.01% / 0.00%**. With a frequency-matching heuristic: 31.0 / 2.9 / 0.07 / 0.02 / 0.00 — [Aldarrab & May, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.15229v1); [ACL Anthology D18-1102](https://aclanthology.org/D18-1102).
- **Aldarrab & May (2021), seq2seq transformer with frequency encoding:** SER 20.0 / 5.56 / 1.66 / 0.34 / 0.00 at lengths 16–256. A **14-language multilingual model** deciphers without explicit language identification, e.g. 5.47% SER on 256 characters of the Borg cipher (Latin) vs 3.91% for a Latin-only model. Without frequency encoding, SER was 71.75% — [arXiv 2012.15229](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.15229v1).
- **LLMs as decipherers (2025):** Claude 3.5 Sonnet reaches EM 0.99 on Caesar. GPT-4o underperforms on Atbash, and "all models exhibit significant challenges in decrypting Medium and Hard encryption methods", attributed to "limited ability to learn and generalize bijections" and tokenization — [Maskey et al., Findings of EMNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.1082.pdf); [arXiv 2505.24621](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24621v2). Earlier: GPT-4 had a 77% success rate on low-complexity ciphers (Noever 2023, cited in the same paper).
- A fine-tuned T5 for English monoalphabetic ciphers reported 73.56% accuracy, rising to 91.96% with correction passes (non-peer-reviewed preprint) — [Preprints.org 2025](https://www.preprints.org/frontend/manuscript/8d1deea92a416f62a1fd8042bb2d808c/download_pub).
- **Applied to PDFs, Krutko/Vol et al. (IAPR DAS 2018):** "Automatic Recovery of Corrupted Font Encoding in PDF Documents Using CNN-based Symbol Recognition with Language Model". CNN OCR of individual glyphs into **homoglyph groups**, then glyph→character mapping resolved with positional info plus linguistic context. Reported **53–77% lower error than Tesseract** and ~**30× faster** on multilingual PDFs — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Automatic-Recovery-of-Corrupted-Font-Encoding-in-Vol-Krutko/e0c6171f58eef6f5b7ef5b388d8c56868e8f5519); numbers as summarized in [CPR, FSI:DI 2026](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- **CPR (Kim et al., Forensic Science International: Digital Investigation 56, 2026):** carving plus a FontDB (glyphorder + name tables from 40 fonts "covering all dataset fonts"; glyph names → Unicode through the Adobe Glyph List). Llama 3.1 8B does **binary O/X "naturalness" validation**, candidate fonts are tried sequentially, and there is a brute-force fallback over the whole FontDB. "Because single-CID validation is unreliable, multiple CIDs associated with the same font tag are jointly verified". Confirmed-font threshold K=5 — [CPR PDF](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- CPR results: **ToUnicode & FontFile both removed → recovery 0.333, CER 0.048, F1 0.939** (all baseline tools 0.000). Resources-object corruption → recovery 1.000, CER 0.092. The authors attribute the higher error to "misclassifications during LLM-based verification" and note that "LLM-based verification may yield false positives or false negatives for single-character CIDs or when contextual information is limited" — [CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- A practitioner reports a tool that reconstructs CID ToUnicode maps using a "large project with 20GB database of symbols" (anecdotal) — [pdfminer.six issue #764](https://github.com/pdfminer/pdfminer.six/issues/764).

### Inferences
- **Replace or back up?** For en/fr/es (and likely ar/hi at the cluster level), decipherment should *back up* database decoding and *become primary when no bundled or system font's GID space fits* (see §3: renumbered subsets from Quartz/macOS, Type3 fonts, unknown fonts). The ciphertext is the code sequence per font slot. The key is code→character, not code→gid→character via an assumed font. That removes the dependence on having the exact font.
- PDF glyph "ciphers" are easier than historical ciphers: word boundaries are usually known (space glyph, TJ offsets, `Td` breaks), and advance widths prune candidate plaintext letters per code (§3). Both shrink the key space, so the effective unicity distance is well below 28 characters. This is inferred from Shannon's formula. No PDF-specific measurement was found.
- A plausible pure-Rust design: per-language character 5–6-gram LM (stupid-backoff or Kneser–Ney, pruned) plus beam search over code→char assignments in frequency order (Nuhn 2013). Seed it with width classes and with cheap "shift/affine" hypotheses (§2). Rescore finished hypotheses with the existing word dictionary (the Hauer 2014 combination). Size is **estimated** at single-digit MB per language for a pruned char 5-gram; runtime is CPU-only, likely milliseconds to seconds per font slot. This is an estimate: Nuhn reports "a fraction of the time" of ILP without absolute numbers.
- A neural char-LM (Kambhatla) would add only small accuracy gains at the lengths typical of a PDF page. It would add an ONNX model plus runtime (rten/tract are pure Rust, so it is allowed, but not needed in v1).
- **LLM validation (CPR-style) conflicts with PDFPundit's constraints.** An 8B model is ~16 GB at fp16 and roughly 4–5 GB 4-bit quantized (estimate). llama.cpp would be C++ FFI (disallowed); candle is pure Rust but the model size is prohibitive for a single-binary tool. CPR's own 33% on the C8-like case suggests it is not the winning ingredient. Keep it as an optional "Pundit+" idea.
- CPR's 33.3% (ToUnicode+FontFile removed) versus REPDF's ≈90% on C8 is a large gap. The corpora differ, but it suggests that REPDF's C8 number depends heavily on the DB containing the corpus's exact fonts (see §3).

### Gaps
- No published decipherment accuracy for **Arabic** (cursive, positional glyphs → homophonic cipher with ~4 glyphs per letter), **Devanagari** (conjunct glyphs → many-to-many), or **Chinese** (thousands of symbols; unicity distance is enormous for a 1:1 key over ~3,000+ characters). The only PDF-specific multilingual numbers are Krutko 2018 (relative to Tesseract) and CPR 2026 (per corruption type, not per language).
- No runtime numbers for beam-search decipherment on commodity CPUs were found.
- The REPDF paper's exact per-language C8 numbers were not re-checked here. The plan states Arabic ~33–40% for C6/C8.

## 2. Glyph-shape and glyph-name methods when the font program survives but ToUnicode is lost; what pdf.js, PDFium, MuPDF, Poppler, pdfminer and PDFBox do

### Takeaway
All mainstream libraries implement the ISO 32000 §9.10.2 ladder: `/ToUnicode` → standard encodings/`/Differences` glyph names via the Adobe Glyph List → `Registry-Ordering-UCS2` maps for CID collections. Beyond that they give up: an identity or byte fallback, `(cid:N)` in pdfminer, "No Unicode mapping" warnings in PDFBox, U+FFFD. Only pdf.js adds glyph-name heuristics (`Gxx`, `uniXXXX`, some ligature names). None does glyph-shape recognition. Shape recognition is proven in research: PDF Mirage (glyph verification "just under 100%" accurate), Krutko 2018 (per-glyph CNN plus LM), and SSIM atlas matching (361/361 glyphs in a Kindle case). It is the right tool when a font program survives (C6, or ToUnicode lost/wrong), and it is pure-Rust feasible.

### Cited Findings
- **pdf.js** (`evaluator.js`): follows §9.10.2. For simple fonts it maps each encoding glyph name through the Adobe Glyph List. It then applies "(undocumented) c) Few heuristics to recognize unknown glyphs. NOTE: Adobe Reader does not do this step, but OSX Preview does": `Gxx` names, `getUnicodeForGlyph` for `uniXXXX`-style names, and non-standard ligature names (`f_h`, `f_t`, `T_h`). For composite fonts it builds `registry-ordering-UCS2` (e.g. Adobe-Japan1-UCS2). Otherwise: "The viewer's choice, just use an identity map" — [pdf.js evaluator.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mozilla/pdf.js/master/src/core/evaluator.js).
- **MuPDF** (`pdf-unicode.c`): uses the embedded ToUnicode stream if present. Otherwise the collection map (Adobe-CNS1/GB1/Japan1/Korea1 → `-UCS2`), otherwise glyph names from the encoding. The code carries an explicit TODO: "synthesize a ToUnicode if it's a freetype font with cmap and/or post tables or if it has glyph names" (not implemented) — [MuPDF pdf-unicode.c](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArtifexSoftware/mupdf/master/source/pdf/pdf-unicode.c).
- **PDFium** (`cpdf_cidfont.cpp`): for CID-keyed fonts, CID→Unicode via built-in `cid2unicode` maps per charset. With `cid_is_gid` it returns the CID as the glyph. There is no shape-based recovery — [PDFium cpdf_cidfont.cpp](https://pdfium.googlesource.com/pdfium/+/refs/heads/main/core/fpdfapi/font/cpdf_cidfont.cpp). Docling (pypdfium2) surfaces unmapped glyphs as `GLYPH<c=...>` or "Caesar-shifted" letters (e.g. `7KH` for `THE`) and has no automatic detection or remediation — [docling issue #2170](https://github.com/docling-project/docling/issues/2170).
- **PDFBox**: returns null when no ToUnicode exists ("there is no way to obtain Unicode for the character") and logs `No Unicode mapping for CID+n`. It treats an Identity-named ToUnicode as identity — [PdfBox-Android PDFont.java](https://github.com/TomRoush/PdfBox-Android/blob/master/library/src/main/java/com/tom_roush/pdfbox/pdmodel/font/PDFont.java); [PDFBOX-4036](https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/PDFBOX-4036).
- **pdfminer.six** emits `(cid:<n>)` for unmapped codes. The maintainer's position: "Not much we can do (sadly)". A user noted that the Adobe `pdf2unicode` mapping resources did not fit because the CIDs "don't follow standard mapping relationship" — [pdfminer.six #764](https://github.com/pdfminer/pdfminer.six/issues/764); [Stefanovitch 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.computel-1.10.pdf).
- **Poppler** drops unmappable glyphs in a Quartz-style symbolic TrueType subset. The Rust library pdf_oxide (Sept 2026) likewise decodes to U+FFFD and warns once per font, after noting that "§9.10.2's three methods all fail here: the ToUnicode is empty, the font is symbolic with no `/Encoding` and no standard names, and the program carries no `post` names" — [pdf_oxide #1520](https://github.com/yfedoseev/pdf_oxide/issues/1520).
- **Glyph-name recovery tool:** `pdf-tounicode-fix` finds fonts without ToUnicode, decompiles CFF (fontTools), reads glyph-name ↔ code encoding, and maps names back to Unicode to write a fresh ToUnicode (aimed at TeX math PDFs) — [thomasSong03/pdf-tounicode-fix](https://github.com/thomasSong03/pdf-tounicode-fix).
- **PDF Mirage (USENIX Security 2017):** proposed defense = "Render each character in the fonts embedded" and OCR each glyph once. It reports "glyph distinction accuracy just under 100%" and "100% content masking attack detection rate", at "roughly constant speed regardless of document length (a tenth of that for full document OCR at 10 pages)". Full-document OCR needed >20% of characters masked before detection — [USENIX paper](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-markwood.pdf); [slides](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/conference/protected-files/usenixsecurity17_slides_markwood.pdf).
- **Krutko et al. 2018** (per-glyph CNN into homoglyph groups plus LM): 53–77% lower error than Tesseract, ~30× faster — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Automatic-Recovery-of-Corrupted-Font-Encoding-in-Vol-Krutko/e0c6171f58eef6f5b7ef5b388d8c56868e8f5519); [CPR related work](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- **Shape matching against a reference atlas (web DRM case, secondary source):** randomized per-request glyph IDs are rendered, perceptually hashed, and matched with SSIM against a reference-font atlas. "361 unique glyphs were matched (100%) with an average SSIM of 0.9527". "OCR on isolated glyphs performs poorly (≈50%), confuses punctuation and look-alike glyphs, and ignores ligatures". Advice: shape ligature strings with HarfBuzz when building the atlas — [HackTricks summary](https://hacktricks.wiki/en/generic-methodologies-and-resources/basic-forensic-methodology/specific-software-file-type-tricks/svg-font-glyph-analysis-and-web-drm-deobfuscation.html). Same-font matching; secondary source.
- **Interactive alignment (Stefanovitch, ComputEL 2022):** the user types visually identifiable words, and the system aligns the typed Unicode with CID sequences (a regex over same-length CID runs, filling the map progressively). On UDHR texts, **complete recovery needed only 4–5% of the text typed**. For scripts whose glyph order differs from Unicode order, the user must type in glyph order and a post-processing swap is applied — [ACL Anthology 2022.computel-1.10](https://aclanthology.org/2022.computel-1.10.pdf); percentage as summarized by [CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- A Rust workspace (`pdf-glyph-mapping`, mostly abandoned) dumps each glyph of each font as a bitmap and extracts Tj/TJ runs per font, to help *manually* associate glyphs with Unicode (Indic focus), then wraps runs in `/ActualText` — [shreevatsa/pdf-glyph-mapping](https://github.com/shreevatsa/pdf-glyph-mapping).

### Inferences
- **Order of operations for any font whose program survives (C6 orphan fonts, "ToUnicode missing/wrong"):**
  1. surviving `/ToUnicode`;
  2. simple-font `/Encoding` plus `/Differences` names → AGL, plus pdf.js-style name heuristics (`uniXXXX`, `uXXXX[XX]`, `gNN`/`GNN`, `f_f_i`-style ligature names);
  3. `CIDSystemInfo` non-Identity ordering → `Adobe-*-UCS2`;
  4. **the embedded font's own `cmap` subtables reversed** (3,1 / 3,10; a (1,0) Mac Roman table only if not symbolic), plus `post`/CFF charset names. This is what MuPDF lists as an unimplemented TODO;
  5. only then shape matching or decipherment.
  Steps 1–4 are deterministic and cheap. PDFPundit's plan jumps straight to database inference.
- **Pure-Rust shape pipeline:** skrifa gives glyph outlines. Rasterize with hayro (already in the stack) or a small rasterizer. Normalize the bounding box. Compare against atlases rendered from candidate fonts (bundled Noto plus system fonts) using SSIM or a perceptual hash, with LM rescoring to resolve homoglyph groups (l/I/1, O/0, rn/m). No C and no ML model needed. An optional tiny per-glyph CNN (Krutko-style) could run via rten/tract. Size unknown; a few MB is plausible (estimate).
- Cross-typeface shape matching (a document in Arial, atlas in Noto Sans) is harder than the same-font matching in the Kindle report. Expect homoglyph confusions and resolve them with the LM (Krutko's homoglyph-group design exists for exactly this).
- Type3 fonts (Chrome emits these, §4) have no font program but do have glyph procedures (content streams). They can be rendered and shape-matched the same way.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head accuracy numbers for cross-font glyph-shape matching per script (Arabic positional forms, Devanagari conjuncts, Han) were found.
- The Poppler glyph-name heuristic list was not fetched directly. Poppler behavior is known here only via pdf_oxide's issue.

## 3. Font identification from surviving evidence: /Widths and /W arrays, FontDescriptor metrics, BaseFont subset names

### Takeaway
Widths are highly discriminative for **identifying the exact font** when the producer kept the original glyph IDs (Skia/Chrome does; Office "Save as"/MS Print to PDF appear to, per CPR). Different families disagree on 51–72 of 73 common characters in a local measurement, so a handful of glyphs fingerprint the font. Widths are **moderately** informative for decoding letters: they cut English letter uncertainty from 4.18 bits to ~2 bits for Arial/Times-metric fonts and to 0.3–0.6 bits for fonts with more varied widths. They carry **zero** information for Han characters (all 1000/1000). The critical design point: a gid→Unicode map is specific to one font file and version. Decoding a document typeset in Arial, Calibri, SimSun or Mangal through **Noto** gid maps is meaningless unless the document was typeset in that Noto build. No published "font identification from PDF widths" study was found. Classic OCR font-ID work (Khoubyari & Hull 1996) is image-based.

### Cited Findings
- **Skia (Chrome's PDF backend) subsets with `HB_SUBSET_FLAGS_RETAIN_GIDS`**, so the CIDs in Chrome-printed PDFs are the original font's glyph IDs — [skia SkPDFSubsetFont.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/skia/main/src/pdf/SkPDFSubsetFont.cpp). Chrome 150 embeds e.g. `AAAAAA+Calibri CID TrueType Identity-H` — [Chromium issue 559097683](https://issues.chromium.org/issues/559097683).
- **macOS Quartz renumbers:** a Word-on-macOS PDF's `AAAAAE+Calibri` subset had 52 glyphs named `glyph00001…`, no GSUB, no `post` names, and only a (1,0) Mac Roman cmap. Its ToUnicode mis-mapped `ti`/`tf`/`ft` ligature glyphs (`operaOons`, `pla[orm`, `Microsoe`). "Every single" library tested gave the same wrong output. The maintainers conclude the only workarounds are OCR or "matching against a known-good copy of the full Calibri font" — [pdf_oxide #444](https://github.com/yfedoseev/pdf_oxide/issues/444). CPR likewise: for macOS-produced files "recovery was infeasible when the CIDs were internally assigned by macOS", but text in fonts with GlyphID-based CIDs was reconstructed — [CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- CPR's FontDB works "particularly when CID source is GlyphID". It stores per-font CID→Unicode columns (e.g. ArialMT, Calibri) built from the `glyphorder` table plus AGL and keyed by PostScript name (nameID 6). Its 40 fonts "cover all dataset fonts" — [CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- A "Caesar-shifted" extraction (`7KH` for `THE`) appears when a subset TrueType is decoded as if code = Unicode — [docling #2170](https://github.com/docling-project/docling/issues/2170). (`T`=0x54 → `7`=0x37 is a constant offset of 29, consistent with retained, alphabetically ordered GIDs; that reading is an inference.)
- PDF/UA-2 requires declared `/Widths` or `/W` to match the embedded program's advance widths within 1/1000 text-space unit — [EqualWeb wiki on PDF/UA-2 §8.4.5.6](https://www.equalweb.com/wiki/pdf-font-glyph-width-consistent).
- For simple fonts, `FirstChar`/`LastChar` bound `/Widths`, and `MissingWidth` covers out-of-range codes. With no `/Encoding`, a Type1 font with Flags bit 6 set uses StandardEncoding — [pdf-association/pdf20examples #7](https://github.com/pdf-association/pdf20examples/issues/7).
- Classic font identification from text images: Khoubyari & Hull (1996) identify the predominant font from repeated function words ("the", "and") — cited in [Chinese font identification paper (2001)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0262885600000822) and [EUSIPCO 2000](https://www.eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2000/SESSIONS/WEDAM/PO4/CR1095.PDF).
- **Local measurement for these notes** (fontTools 4.66 on fonts present in the research container; advances scaled to 1000/em as in PDF `/W`; English letter frequencies approximated from the standard Wikipedia table). Own computation, not a published source:
  - Liberation Sans (Arial-metric): 7 width classes for a–z, e.g. {556: abdeghnopqu}, {500: cksvxyz}, {222: ijl}, {278: ft}; 3/26 letters uniquely identified; H(letter) 4.18 → H(letter|width) **2.10 bits**.
  - Liberation Serif (Times-metric): 7 classes, e.g. {500: bdghknopquvxy}; → **1.88 bits**.
  - DejaVu Sans: 16 classes, 12/26 unique → **0.57 bits**. FreeSans: 21 classes, 18/26 unique → **0.28 bits**.
  - Across 73 common characters, font pairs differ (by >1/1000 em) in **51–72** characters.
  - WenQuanYi Zen Hei: all **20,940 Han characters have width 1000**.
  - DejaVu Sans Arabic: 37 base letters → 19 width classes; 140 presentation forms → 55 classes.

### Inferences
- **The planned Noto-gmap decoding has a GID-space problem.** PDFPundit's §5.2/§18.2 decodes `code → gid → gmap(Noto).unicode(gid)`. For Identity-H fonts with retained GIDs, the gids belong to the *original* font (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, SimSun, Microsoft YaHei, Mangal/Nirmala UI, Arabic Typesetting…). Looking them up in Noto's glyph order yields garbage, unless the source document used that exact Noto build. REPDF and CPR both succeed because their DBs contain the corpus's actual fonts (CPR explicitly). **Step 0 for PDFPundit: audit the REPDF corpus's intact originals for `/BaseFont` names and font versions.** If they are Office/Windows fonts, a Noto-only DB cannot reproduce REPDF's C8 ≈90%.
- **Fixes, in order:**
  - (a) Runtime **system-font gmaps**: fontique enumeration is already planned for the output path. Extend it to *inference*: read cmap/hmtx/glyphorder from installed fonts with read-fonts and cache `.gmap`s. Windows machines, where most corpus files likely originated, will have the right fonts. No font bytes are redistributed.
  - (b) Optionally ship **metadata-only tables** (gid→Unicode plus advance widths, keyed by PostScript name, font version and a width-vector hash) for the most common Office/OS fonts. Whether such tables are free of font-license restrictions needs legal review.
  - (c) Bundled Noto remains the **output substitute** and the decoder only for Noto-typeset documents.
- **Width fingerprint for exact identification (C7/C8, retained GIDs):** for each candidate font, check `W[cid] == round(hmtx[cid]·1000/upm)` over all used CIDs. With font pairs disagreeing on ~70–99% of glyphs, a match on a dozen distinct CIDs is very strong evidence of the same font *and GID space*. This is stronger than any text score and works on 3-character headings where dictionary scoring cannot. Metric-compatible clones (Liberation vs Arial; widths identical by design — well-known, not re-verified this session) match widths but have **different GIDs**. So a width match identifies the metric family, and the GID check (does the decoded text pass the LM?) confirms the exact font.
- **Widths as decipherment constraints (renumbered GIDs, unknown fonts):** each code's width restricts it to one width class in the candidate metric family. That is ~2 bits of residual uncertainty per Latin letter for Arial/Times-metric fonts (from 4.18). This makes short-run decipherment much more feasible and gives a per-code consistency check. Not useful for Han. Moderately useful for Arabic positional forms.
- **Cheap "shift" hypotheses:** retained-GID Latin fonts usually keep A–Z and a–z in contiguous alphabetical runs. Testing constant-offset or piecewise-offset decoders (key space of hundreds; Caesar unicity distance ≈2 characters per Practical Cryptography) before full decipherment will solve many unknown-but-standard-ordered fonts instantly. This is an inference from the `7KH` example.
- **Subset prefixes and names:** `ABCDEF+` prefixes are random. Strip them. The remainder (`Calibri`, `ArialMT`) is the best key into system and metadata tables. For Print-to-PDF names (`CIDFont+F1`, `F1`), ignore the name and rely on widths plus decoding.
- FontDescriptor metrics (`/Flags` serif/symbolic/fixed-pitch, `/ItalicAngle`, `/StemV`, `/CapHeight`, `/XHeight`, `/AvgWidth`, `/FontBBox`) survive C7/C8 and are cheap priors to rank candidate families before width matching. No published discriminability numbers were found (gap).

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of font identification from PDF `/Widths`/`/W` arrays or FontDescriptor metrics was found.
- It was not verified whether Microsoft Print to PDF and Word "Save as PDF" retain original GIDs. CPR's 100%/99.3% FontDB recovery on those producers is consistent with GlyphID-based CIDs but does not state it outright.
- The legal status of shipping gid→Unicode and width tables derived from proprietary fonts was not researched.

## 4. Script-specific issues (Arabic, Devanagari/Hindi, CJK) and why "Print to PDF" output is hard

### Takeaway
Arabic and Devanagari break the one-glyph-one-character assumption in both directions. Arabic uses positional forms, obligatory ligatures (lam-alef) and visual-order runs. Devanagari uses conjunct glyphs mapping to several code points and visual-order reordering (i-matra before its consonant) that ToUnicode cannot express without `/ActualText`. CJK is easy when the font is CID-keyed to a standard collection (Adobe-GB1 → UCS2 maps are deterministic) and hard for Identity-H subsets without the exact font: widths are useless, and 1:1 decipherment over thousands of symbols needs a lot of text. "Print to PDF" is hard because of page-scoped generic font tags (MS Print to PDF: `F1` = Arial on page 1 and Times on page 2), renamed fonts (`CIDFont+F1`), Type3 fallbacks (Chrome), renumbered subsets (Quartz), and content and font streams placed near EOF.

### Cited Findings
- **Arabic ligatures and order:** extraction libraries return Arabic runs reversed (visual order), but a lam-alef ligature glyph mapped to "uni0644 uni0627" is **not** reversed, corrupting words after bidi. One workaround is to substitute the U+FEFB presentation-form ligature, reverse, then NFKD. A maintainer notes that proper support "would actually need to call a text shaping component like HarfBuzz" — [PyMuPDF #2199](https://github.com/pymupdf/PyMuPDF/issues/2199); [Stack Overflow 75280067](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/75280067/rtl-arabic-ligatures-problem-when-extracting-text-from-pdf).
- **Devanagari:** in Noto Sans Devanagari, the word प्राप्त is laid out with four glyphs. One glyph maps to PA + VIRAMA, and ToUnicode maps "often" miss such non-trivial cases. "Even after the mapping is fixed … the glyph ids are laid out in visual order while Unicode text is in phonetic order", and text needing complex layout "cannot be copied correctly from PDFs, unless annotated with `ActualText`" — [shreevatsa/pdf-glyph-mapping](https://github.com/shreevatsa/pdf-glyph-mapping). Stefanovitch's interactive method likewise needs a post-processing swap for scripts whose glyph order differs from character order — [ComputEL 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.computel-1.10.pdf).
- PDF generators that map Unicode → glyphs without GSUB/GPOS break halant conjuncts, matra reordering and ligatures (क्ष, त्र, ज्ञ) — [dart_pdf #1907](https://github.com/DavBfr/dart_pdf/issues/1907). `/ActualText` is the standard way to give extractable characters that differ from the glyphs' default mapping — [PDF Association technique G2_06](https://pdfa.org/techniques-for-accessible-pdf/actualtext-provides-correct-extractable-characters-in-place-of-ocr-errors/UA1_Tpdf-G2_06).
- **Latin ligatures without code points** (`ti`, `tf`, `ft` in Calibri) need multi-character ToUnicode destinations (`<00740069>`). ISO 32000 §9.10.3 Example 2 shows this; Quartz fails to do it — [pdf_oxide #444](https://github.com/yfedoseev/pdf_oxide/issues/444).
- **CJK collections:** MuPDF loads `Adobe-GB1-UCS2`, `-CNS1-`, `-Japan1-`, `-Korea1-` for collection-keyed CID fonts. pdf.js builds `registry-ordering-UCS2` per §9.10.2 — [MuPDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArtifexSoftware/mupdf/master/source/pdf/pdf-unicode.c); [pdf.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mozilla/pdf.js/master/src/core/evaluator.js). Adobe publishes these "mapping resources for PDF" (CID → Unicode, distinct from CMap resources that map codes → CIDs), updated per Supplement — [adobe-type-tools/mapping-resources-pdf](https://github.com/adobe-type-tools/mapping-resources-pdf). For Identity-H fonts the collection maps do **not** apply, since CIDs are font-specific glyph IDs — [pdfminer.six #764](https://github.com/pdfminer/pdfminer.six/issues/764).
- **Microsoft Print to PDF:** "font tags that reference different fonts on each page. That is, the same F1 tag may point to 'Arial' on page 1 and 'Times New Roman' on page 2"; CIDs appear only as 4-hex-digit `<…>` strings. CPR's page-indexed (`pidx`) FontCMap handles this and reached **100% recovery (CER 0.030)** on MS Print to PDF files, 99.3% on MS "Save as", 85.4% on macOS Document Editor, and 92.4% on Adobe Acrobat — [CPR Table 3](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf). REPDF (per PDFPundit's plan): Print-to-PDF font names like `CIDFont+F1` "change, defeating name lookup", and C10 Print-to-PDF truncation recovery is ~35% because content and font streams sit near EOF — `/home/user/PDFPundit/nimbalyst-local/plans/pdfpundit-desktop-app.md`.
- **Chrome/Skia print:** normally `AAAAAA+Font` Identity-H CIDFontType2 subsets with retained GIDs ([Skia](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/skia/main/src/pdf/SkPDFSubsetFont.cpp)). When a font's OS/2 `fsType` restricts embedding or subsetting, Chrome "used only the outlines to create a stripped down minimal Type3 font" — [Chromium 40189033](https://issues.chromium.org/issues/40189033). Chrome 151 regressed to Type3 fonts with corrupted `/Widths` for fonts with embedded bitmap strikes — [Chromium 559097683](https://issues.chromium.org/issues/559097683). A missing ToUnicode entry for an `fi` ligature was also reported — [Chromium 41473143](https://issues.chromium.org/issues/41473143).
- **Adobe Acrobat output:** mixes `( )` literal byte strings (octal escapes) and `< >` 4-digit hex CIDs, with page-scoped font tags — [CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).

### Inferences
- **PDFPundit's Arabic plan is necessary but not sufficient.** Shaping each *character* in isolated/initial/medial/final context covers positional forms, but misses:
  - (i) lam-alef and other ligatures (one gid → two or more characters);
  - (ii) contextual alternates and marks (harakat usually zero-width, positioned via GPOS);
  - (iii) run order: content streams usually hold Arabic in visual order, so reverse per run, except within ligature clusters, before LM scoring.
  **Recommendation:** at build time, shape whole dictionary *words* with harfrust and use its cluster mapping to record `glyph-sequence → text` pairs. Store `gid → String` (not `u32`). Decode with a longest-match glyph-n-gram lexicon plus a character LM on the logical-order output.
- **Hindi needs the same word-level shaping:** conjuncts are many-to-one, and the i-matra/reph reordering must be undone when decoding. For output, a ToUnicode CMap cannot express reordering. The rebuilt text layer should wrap runs in `/ActualText` marked-content spans. PDFPundit's `build_tounicode(used: &BTreeMap<u16, char>)` signature can express neither multi-codepoint glyphs nor reordering. Change it to `BTreeMap<u16, String>` (bfchar supports multi-codepoint UTF-16BE destinations), with optional ActualText spans.
- **Chinese:**
  - If `CIDSystemInfo` ordering is GB1/CNS1/Japan1/Korea1 (not Identity), CID→Unicode is deterministic via Adobe's mapping resources. Bundle them after checking the license; the repo's license was not checked here.
  - For Identity-H without the exact font, widths are useless (all 1000), so recovery must come from the right font's gmap (system SimSun, Microsoft YaHei or DengXian on Windows), or from shape matching when the program survives. The planned character-bigram scorer is appropriate for *ranking candidate fonts*, but 1:1 decipherment of Han from scratch is unrealistic at page-scale text lengths (inference; no published numbers).
- **"Print to PDF" in PDFPundit:**
  - (1) Treat font slot names as **page-scoped**. Do inference and assignment per (page, slot), then merge slots whose code-usage and width vectors agree.
  - (2) Ignore `CIDFont+Fn` names.
  - (3) Detect Type3 fonts and route them to glyph-procedure shape matching.
  - (4) Expect that a truncated Print-to-PDF file (C10) may have lost font objects entirely, which falls back to C8-style decoding.

### Gaps
- No quantitative study of extraction accuracy for Hindi/Arabic PDFs by producer was found.
- Whether Microsoft Print to PDF emits ToUnicode, and whether it retains GIDs, was not confirmed from a primary source.
- The license of Adobe `mapping-resources-pdf` was not checked.

## 5. Full-page OCR as a fallback: pure-Rust options, accuracy, and aligning OCR text with glyph-code sequences

### Takeaway
OCR can only help when the page renders with the correct glyph shapes: C6 (font program survives), ToUnicode lost or wrong, or Type3. It **cannot** help C7/C8, where the font program is gone and a rendered page shows substitute glyphs. Where it applies, OCR is best used as an **alignment oracle**: OCR a sample of lines, align characters to code sequences, and majority-vote a code→char map. It should not replace the text layer. Pure-Rust options exist: ocrs is Latin-only and early preview; PaddleOCR ONNX models run through tract (pure Rust) and cover Chinese, Arabic and Devanagari with ~5–8 MB models. Tesseract, MNN and onnxruntime-based crates are C/C++ and disallowed.

### Cited Findings
- **ocrs** (Robert Knight, ex-Tesseract contributor): pure-Rust OCR whose PyTorch-trained models are exported to ONNX and run on the **RTen** engine. "Currently recognizes the Latin alphabet only (eg. English)"; "early preview. Expect more errors than commercial OCR engines" — [robertknight/ocrs](https://github.com/robertknight/ocrs). Its models are trained only on openly licensed datasets such as HierText (CC-BY-SA 4.0) — [ocrs-models](https://github.com/robertknight/ocrs-models). RTen is "End-to-end Rust", CPU-only with AVX2/AVX-512/Neon/WASM SIMD, and supports int8-quantized models — [robertknight/rten](https://github.com/robertknight/rten).
- **pure-onnx-ocr:** "A Pure Rust OCR pipeline that mirrors the PaddleOCR DBNet + SVTR stack … without any C/C++ dependencies", running on `tract-onnx` — [docs.rs pure_onnx_ocr](https://docs.rs/pure-onnx-ocr/latest/pure_onnx_ocr/); a sync variant was published Jan 2026 — [crates.io pure-onnx-ocr-sync](https://crates.io/crates/pure-onnx-ocr-sync).
- **Disallowed by the no-C rule:** `rusto-rs` and `ocr-rs` use Alibaba's MNN (C++) — [rusto-rs](https://github.com/byrizki/rusto-rs); [ocr-rs](https://lib.rs/crates/ocr-rs); [rust-paddle-ocr](https://github.com/zibo-chen/rust-paddle-ocr). `franken_ocr` is pure Rust but runs vision-language models (large) — [franken_ocr](https://github.com/Dicklesworthstone/franken_ocr).
- **PaddleOCR model sizes and accuracy** (line accuracy: a line counts as wrong if any character is wrong):
  - `devanagari_PP-OCRv3_mobile_rec`: **96.44%**, **7.9 MB** — [HF](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/devanagari_PP-OCRv3_mobile_rec).
  - `arabic_PP-OCRv3_mobile_rec`: **73.55%**, **7.8 MB** — [HF](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/arabic_PP-OCRv3_mobile_rec).
  - `arabic_PP-OCRv5_mobile_rec`: **81.27%** — [HF](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/arabic_PP-OCRv5_mobile_rec/blob/main/README.md).
  - `PP-OCRv5_mobile_det.onnx`: **4.75 MB** — [HF](https://huggingface.co/PaddleOcrNet/PaddleOcrNet-models/blob/main/PP-OCRv5_mobile_det.onnx).
  - PP-OCRv5 multilingual recognition covers 106–109 languages, including Arabic and Devanagari, with a "2M parameters" model. Default PP-OCRv5 covers Simplified/Traditional Chinese, English, Japanese and Pinyin. PP-OCRv6 (2026) tiers: tiny 1.5M / small 7.7M / medium 34.5M, +5.1% recognition over v5 — [PaddleOCR README](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR); [PP-OCRv5 multilingual docs](https://www.paddleocr.ai/latest/en/version3.x/algorithm/PP-OCRv5/PP-OCRv5_multi_languages.html).
- **Full-page OCR vs glyph-level checking:** in PDF Mirage, full-document OCR had a 7% character-difference noise floor and only detected content masking once >20% of characters were masked. Per-glyph verification cost ~1/10 of full-document OCR at 10 pages — [USENIX 2017](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity17/sec17-markwood.pdf).
- **Alignment precedents:** Stefanovitch 2022 aligns user-typed Unicode with CID sequences to build the map incrementally, with full recovery from 4–5% of text — [ComputEL 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.computel-1.10.pdf). Knight & Brailsford (DocEng 2016) replaced an inadequate hidden text layer with one aligned from a "truth text" guided by OCR coordinates — [Semantic Scholar listing](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Automatic-Recovery-of-Corrupted-Font-Encoding-in-Vol-Krutko/e0c6171f58eef6f5b7ef5b388d8c56868e8f5519). pdf-inspector (pure Rust, lopdf-based) "automatically flags broken font encodings so callers can fall back to OCR" and gives per-page OCR routing — [docmost/pdf-inspector2](https://github.com/docmost/pdf-inspector2).

### Inferences
- **Why OCR is an alignment oracle rather than a text source:** every code occurs many times, so noisy OCR plus a majority vote per code gives a clean map. Each OCR'd line is aligned to the code sequence of the same line (known positions from the content stream) with edit-distance alignment; votes accumulate per code; OCR stops once every used code has ≥k consistent votes (sampling lines, not whole pages). The resulting map then decodes the entire document exactly, with correct spacing and order, and yields a proper `/ToUnicode`. Arabic and Hindi need cluster-level (glyph-n-gram) votes and the reordering noted in §4.
- **Pure-Rust packaging:** use tract or rten plus PaddleOCR ONNX models: det ~4.75 MB plus rec ~8 MB per script, ~30–40 MB for en/fr/es + ar + hi + zh (estimate; the PP-OCRv4 zh rec model is 10.9 MB, so ~35–45 MB is safer). ocrs alone covers only en/fr/es. For a v1 single binary, OCR is a reasonable **optional download or feature-gated component**; the plan schedules it as the optional, feature-gated M9 (it was parked in "Pundit+" until 2026-10-04; see [outlined_text_and_ocr.md §5](outlined_text_and_ocr.md)). Per-glyph rendering plus shape matching (§2) gives most of the benefit without any model.
- **Rendering for OCR:** hayro can rasterize the page, but only with the original font program (C6 and ToUnicode-lost cases). For C8 there is nothing correct to render.

### Gaps
- No published character error rates for ocrs, and no CPU latency numbers for tract-run PaddleOCR on typical pages, were found.
- The Chinese PP-OCRv5 mobile rec ONNX size was not verified. The PP-OCRv4 Chinese/English rec ONNX (`ch_PP-OCRv4_rec_infer.onnx`, as packaged in RapidOCR 1.4.4) is 10.9 MB, measured 2026-10-04; see [outlined_text_and_ocr.md §3](outlined_text_and_ocr.md).
- No published study of OCR-to-glyph-code alignment accuracy was found beyond Stefanovitch's user-typed variant.

## 6. Measuring confidence and deciding when to route to a human

### Takeaway
PDFPundit's planned confidence (`0.5·hit_rate + 0.5·relative margin`, auto-accept at ≥0.35 with hit ≥0.5) is heuristic and scale-dependent. It ignores two things the literature makes central: the **amount of text** (error falls sharply between 16 and 64 characters) and **independent non-text evidence** (exact width or name matches). Use a length-aware, calibrated posterior over candidate decodings. Validate jointly over many codes, never per single glyph. Where a human is needed, ask them to type what they see rather than only pick a font: 4–5% of the text is enough for full recovery.

### Cited Findings
- Decipherment error depends strongly on length: SER 26.8% at 16 characters, 5.8% at 32, 0.07% at 64 (Kambhatla 2018), and 20.0 / 5.56 / 1.66 / 0.34% at 16/32/64/128 (Aldarrab & May) — [arXiv 2012.15229](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.15229v1).
- Below the unicity distance (~28 characters for English simple substitution) multiple plausible decryptions exist, e.g. the six-letter ciphertext "FJKFPO" has many valid English decodings ("thatis", "season", "oxford") — [Practical Cryptography](http://www.practicalcryptography.com/cryptanalysis/text-characterisation/statistics/).
- CPR: "single-CID validation is unreliable, [so] multiple CIDs associated with the same font tag are jointly verified". It requires K=5 successes before a font is confirmed, and notes LLM false positives/negatives "for single-character CIDs or when contextual information is limited" — [CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- Human-in-the-loop: typing visually identifiable words, with the system choosing which words to request to minimize input, gives full recovery from 4–5% of the text — [Stefanovitch 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.computel-1.10.pdf).
- REPDF itself calls for "an interactive step in which the user reviews the recovered text and selects the most contextually appropriate font" for languages like Arabic — `/home/user/PDFPundit/nimbalyst-local/plans/pdfpundit-desktop-app.md`.

### Inferences
- **Score:** replace the word-hit score with (a) the per-character log-likelihood of the decoded text under a character n-gram LM for the candidate language, plus (b) a word-dictionary bonus, (c) a width-consistency log-likelihood (§3), and (d) name and descriptor priors. Convert to a posterior over candidate (font, language, decoding) hypotheses with a softmax over total log-likelihoods. Total, not normalized, because longer text should produce more peaked posteriors. That makes "margin" meaningful and length-aware automatically.
- **Calibration:** create a synthetic calibration set by taking intact PDFs, stripping ToUnicode and FontFile, and recording whether the top hypothesis is correct. Fit a temperature or isotonic calibration of the posterior, then pick the auto-accept threshold for a target precision (e.g. ≥99% of auto-accepted slots correct). Standard selective-prediction practice; no PDF-specific source found.
- **Routing rules (suggested):**
  - Auto-accept when an exact width-vector match plus LM agreement is found, even for short text.
  - Otherwise require calibrated p ≥ threshold **and** ≥ ~64–100 decoded characters per font slot for alphabetic scripts.
  - Flag slots below the unicity distance (<~30 characters) as "ambiguous" regardless of score.
  - Keep **per-code** confidence (vote counts, LM posterior per code) and highlight low-confidence codes in the preview.
- **Better human step:** instead of only "pick one of 5 fonts", show the glyphs (rendered from the surviving program, or the substitute-font rendering plus width hints) and let the user type 1–3 words. Solve the rest by alignment plus decipherment. This fits the planned TUI modal and should lift the Arabic and "Print to PDF" weak spots far more than font picking.

### Gaps
- No published calibration study for PDF font-inference confidence was found.
- The suggested thresholds are inferences, to be tuned on the M7 benchmark.

## 7. Judgment of PDFPundit's planned C6–C8 approach, and ranked improvements

### Takeaway
The plan faithfully re-implements REPDF, and its extras (harfrust-shaped Arabic maps, a Chinese bigram table, ToUnicode-as-dictionary, an interactive font pick) are sensible. Four gaps are likely to cap accuracy well below REPDF on real documents:
1. **GID-space mismatch.** Noto gid maps cannot decode documents typeset in other fonts.
2. **No deterministic §9.10.2 ladder or C6 re-linking step** before statistical inference.
3. **Surviving `/W`/`/Widths` evidence goes unused.**
4. **One-glyph-one-char data structures** that cannot represent Arabic/Hindi ligatures, conjuncts and reordering.

Decipherment and shape matching give principled, pure-Rust fallbacks for fonts not in any database.

### Cited Findings
- Plan scoring and thresholds: `score = Σ 2.0·len(w) … − 3.0·unmapped …`; `conf = 0.5·hit + 0.5·margin`; auto-accept at `conf ≥ 0.35 && hit ≥ 0.5`; `.gmap` record = `gid:u16, unicode:u32, width:u16, source:u8`; `build_tounicode(used:&BTreeMap<u16,char>)`; inference "always draws candidates from the bundled DB" — `/home/user/PDFPundit/nimbalyst-local/plans/pdfpundit-technical-design.md` §5.2, §5.5, §18.1–18.3.
- Retained-GID producers (Skia) versus renumbering producers (Quartz); FontDB success only "when CID source is GlyphID" — [Skia](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google/skia/main/src/pdf/SkPDFSubsetFont.cpp); [pdf_oxide #444](https://github.com/yfedoseev/pdf_oxide/issues/444); [CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- Decipherment accuracy by length — [Nuhn 2013](https://aclanthology.org/P13-1154); [Kambhatla 2018 via Aldarrab & May](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.15229v1).
- Page-scoped font tags in MS Print to PDF, and CPR's 100% recovery with page-indexed maps — [CPR](https://dfrws.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/CPR-Corrupted-PDF-recovery-algorithm-for_2026_Forensic-Science-Internationa.pdf).
- Library ladders (§9.10.2) and MuPDF's unimplemented cmap/post synthesis — [pdf.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mozilla/pdf.js/master/src/core/evaluator.js); [MuPDF](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ArtifexSoftware/mupdf/master/source/pdf/pdf-unicode.c).
- Interactive alignment with 4–5% of the text typed — [Stefanovitch 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.computel-1.10.pdf).

### Inferences — ranked changes to PDFPundit's C6–C8 approach
Ranked by expected accuracy gain × feasibility under the pure-Rust constraint. "Pure Rust" means no C and no large model unless flagged.

1. **Fix the GID-space assumption (C8, and C6 when names are lost). Highest impact.**
   - Step 0: audit the `/BaseFont` names and versions in REPDF's intact originals.
   - Build `.gmap`s at runtime from **installed system fonts** (fontique + read-fonts; already in the stack), keyed by PostScript name, version and a width-vector hash.
   - Optionally ship metadata-only gid→Unicode/width tables for common Office/OS fonts (legal review first).
   - Keep Noto for output substitution and for Noto-typeset documents.
   Pure Rust. Without this, a Noto-only DB likely cannot reproduce REPDF's C8 numbers on Office-produced files.
2. **Implement C6 as re-linking and assignment, per (page, slot).** Match each content-stream slot (`/F1` on page p) to carved orphan font objects using:
   - code-range coverage (`FirstChar`/`LastChar`, `/W` keys ⊇ used codes);
   - width consistency;
   - the text decoded through each carved font's *own surviving* ToUnicode, scored by the LM.
   Treat names as page-scoped (MS Print to PDF reuses `F1` for different fonts per page; CPR got 100% this way). Pure Rust, cheap, and it avoids statistical font inference altogether when the font objects survive.
3. **Deterministic §9.10.2 ladder before inference:** ToUnicode → `/Encoding`/`/Differences` + AGL + name heuristics (`uniXXXX`, `uXXXX`, `gNN`, ligature names) → `Adobe-{GB1,CNS1,Japan1,Korea1}-UCS2` (bundle Adobe mapping resources, license to check) → embedded program's cmap/post/CFF charset when the program survives. Pure Rust, data only.
4. **Use `/W`/`/Widths` as first-class evidence.**
   - (a) Exact width-vector matching to identify the font and GID space. This decides short slots where text scoring cannot.
   - (b) Width-class constraints in decoding and decipherment: ~4.2 → ~2 bits per Latin letter for Arial/Times-metric fonts, lower for others; useless for Han.
   - (c) FontDescriptor flags and metrics as priors.
   Pure Rust.
5. **Add a decipherment back-end** for fonts with no matching database or system font (renumbered subsets from Quartz/macOS, Type3, exotic fonts):
   - character 5–6-gram LM plus beam search (Nuhn 2013), seeded by frequency, width classes and cheap constant- or piecewise-offset hypotheses (the "7KH" case);
   - rescore with word dictionaries (Hauer 2014).
   Expected accuracy for alphabetic scripts: ~5% SER at 32 characters and ≈0 at ≥64–128 characters per font (literature). Pure Rust; LM tables a few MB per language (estimate). A neural LM is optional (rten/tract, small).
6. **Replace word-hit scoring and ad-hoc confidence with a calibrated, length-aware posterior.** LM log-likelihood + dictionary bonus + width likelihood + priors → softmax posterior. Calibrate on synthetic strip-ToUnicode/FontFile corruptions. Keep per-code confidence. Refuse auto-accept below ~30 decoded characters unless width and name evidence is exact. Pure Rust.
7. **Many-to-many glyph maps and output text layer for ar/hi (and Latin ligatures).**
   - Shape whole dictionary words with harfrust and keep cluster mappings, so the `.gmap` stores `gid → String` plus a glyph-n-gram lexicon.
   - Reverse Arabic runs, keeping ligature clusters intact.
   - Undo Devanagari visual reordering.
   - Emit multi-codepoint `bfchar` entries (`BTreeMap<u16, String>`) and `/ActualText` spans where ToUnicode cannot express the order.
   Pure Rust. This directly targets REPDF's weakest cells (Arabic C6/C8 ~33–40%).
8. **Replace or augment "pick a font" with "type what you see".** Stefanovitch-style alignment prompts (the system chooses which words to ask for; ~4–5% of the text gives full recovery), feeding the decipherment and alignment solver. The same modal can still offer top-k font candidates. Pure Rust and TUI-friendly. Likely the biggest lever for the remaining Arabic and "Print to PDF" failures.
9. **Glyph-shape matching when a font program or Type3 procedures survive** (C6 orphans, ToUnicode lost or wrong, Quartz ligature bugs). Render outlines (skrifa + hayro), match against atlases from system and bundled fonts (SSIM or perceptual hash), and resolve homoglyphs with the LM. Optionally add a small per-glyph CNN via rten/tract (Krutko 2018 reports 53–77% lower error than Tesseract). Pure Rust; the model is optional.
10. **CJK specifics:** collection maps for non-Identity orderings (item 3). For Identity-H, rely on exact-font gmaps from system fonts (SimSun, Microsoft YaHei, DengXian…) confirmed by the bigram LM. Do not attempt 1:1 Han decipherment from scratch. Widths give no signal.
11. **OCR fallback, post-v1 and feature-gated:** only for cases where the page renders with correct glyphs. Use it as an alignment oracle that fills the code map, never as text replacement. Pure-Rust route: tract or rten + PaddleOCR ONNX (det ~4.75 MB + rec ~8 MB per script; Devanagari 96.4% and Arabic 73.6–81.3% line accuracy); ocrs for Latin only. **Flag:** Tesseract, MNN and onnxruntime crates are C/C++ and disallowed.
12. **Do not adopt CPR-style LLM validation in v1.** Llama 3.1 8B means multi-GB weights and would need C++ (llama.cpp) or a very heavy pure-Rust candle build. CPR's own ToUnicode+FontFile-removed recovery was 33%. At most, an opt-in "Pundit+" add-on.

### Gaps
- Whether REPDF's corpus fonts are Noto or Office/system fonts is unknown to this researcher. It decides how damaging gap #1 is.
- The expected gains of items 1–9 are not quantified by any source against the REPDF corpus. They must be measured in PDFPundit's M7 harness, ideally reporting per language × producer (Save As vs Print to PDF) × corruption class.
